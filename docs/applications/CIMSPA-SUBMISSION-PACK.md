# APPLICATION CONTENT

## 1. Provider profile

Concussion Education Australia (CEA) is a specialist provider of concussion education for health and exercise professionals. CEA builds single-condition programmes to depth rather than broad general-practice curricula: every product the organisation publishes concerns the recognition, assessment, rehabilitation and return-to-activity management of concussion and mild traumatic brain injury.

CEA's programmes are authored in-house by a registered clinician, referenced to the primary peer-reviewed literature, and written to explicit scope-of-practice boundaries so that non-medical practitioners can act confidently inside their lane and escalate correctly outside it.

Concussion Rehab Mastery is CEA's programme for the exercise and physical-activity workforce. It is the product submitted here for endorsement.

## 2. Course description

**Concussion Rehab Mastery** trains exercise professionals to deliver the exercise-based treatment of concussion that international consensus now places first-line.

Until recently, concussion management meant rest until symptoms resolved. That changed. The 6th International Conference on Concussion in Sport (Amsterdam, October 2022; Patricios et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 57(11), 695–711) concluded that **sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise, prescribed from a heart-rate threshold derived by a graded exercise test, is first-line treatment** — prescribed early rather than after failure of rest. The graded exercise test that produces that threshold is the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), developed and validated by Leddy, Willer and colleagues at the University at Buffalo.

That shift moved the centre of gravity of concussion rehabilitation directly into the competence of the exercise professional. Prescribing an individualised aerobic dose from a measured heart-rate threshold, monitoring symptom response in real time, and progressing load against objective criteria is exercise-prescription work. Prescribed individualised exercise on this model improves recovery by approximately 4.6 days on average and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms.

The barrier is not capability, and it is not a lack of concussion education. It is that concussion education across the relevant professions was built before 2022 and therefore teaches the pre-shift model well. A survey of 1,272 physiotherapists illustrates the pattern: 70% already held concussion training and averaged 13/15 on knowledge, with the gaps concentrated in return-to-play confidence and in knowing when graded exertional training is indicated. What is missing is not awareness of concussion but the concussion-specific exertion protocol, the threshold-derived prescription, the scope boundary and the escalation pathway.

The programme closes that gap across eight modules: the pathophysiology that explains why exercise works and why it must stay below threshold; recognition and red-flag triage; the full BCTT and Buffalo Concussion Bike Test protocols; conversion of the heart-rate threshold into a complete FITT prescription; phenotype-specific rehabilitation across the seven recognised concussion phenotypes; the six-stage graded return-to-sport strategy; persistent post-concussion symptoms and the stalled case; and the documentation and inter-professional communication that protect the practitioner and close the loop with the referring clinician.

A defining feature of the design is **scope discipline**. Every module states what the exercise professional does and does not do. Diagnosis is a medical act. Return-to-contact clearance is a medical act. The practitioner trained by this programme implements graded exertion, measures it, documents it, and hands a prepared athlete back to the clinician who holds the clearance decision. Learners are assessed on that boundary as rigorously as on the protocol itself.

## 4. Delivery mode

Wholly online, asynchronous and self-paced, delivered through CEA's own learning platform. Learners authenticate individually; progress is tracked per module; no cohort scheduling or live attendance is required, so the programme is accessible across UK time zones and shift patterns.

Each module combines structured written instruction, worked clinical calculation (heart-rate threshold conversion, target-band derivation, load progression), protocol walkthroughs, decision frameworks and copyable documentation artifacts. Learners may pause and resume without losing progress. Access is retained after completion so the material functions as an ongoing reference in practice.

On successful completion the learner receives a certificate stating the module titles, the assessed instructional hours and the CPD points earned. On endorsement, the CIMSPA endorsement mark and endorsement reference would be carried on that certificate and on the product page.

## 5. Assessment

Assessment is **formative-to-summative and distributed**, not a single terminal exam.

- **87 assessment questions in total**, allocated across the eight modules in proportion to module weight.
- Questions are delivered **within each module**, at the point of learning, so that a learner cannot advance past a protocol step they have not understood.
- **80% pass mark** applies at module level.
- Questions test protocol accuracy (per-minute BCTT increments, termination criteria), physiological reasoning (why the threshold represents the autonomic/cerebrovascular ceiling), clinical decision-making (progress, regress, refer) and — heavily — **scope discipline**, including red-flag escalation and the boundary at return-to-contact clearance.
- Each item returns a written rationale on answer, so incorrect responses are a teaching event rather than a score.
- Certification is issued only on completion of every module at or above the pass mark.

## 6. Learning outcomes

The programme carries **51 assessed learning objectives**. They are reproduced in full below, by module, as they appear in the delivered content.

### Module 1 — Concussion for the Exercise Physiologist
On completion the learner will be able to:
- Define concussion as a functional brain injury, distinguish it from structural traumatic brain injury, and explain why standard neuroimaging is typically normal.
- Describe the biomechanical mechanisms of concussion — rotational, linear, coup-contrecoup and diffuse axonal injury — and explain why rotational forces are considered most damaging.
- Trace the neurometabolic cascade from mechanoporation through ionic flux, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase overdrive, hyperglycolysis, Ca²⁺-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation to recovery — and connect each phase to clinical symptoms.
- Explain cerebral blood-flow dysregulation after concussion, including impaired autoregulation, neurovascular uncoupling and reduced cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity, and describe how each contributes to symptom provocation during exertion.
- Describe post-concussion autonomic dysfunction — sympathovagal imbalance, blunted baroreflex sensitivity and reduced HRV — and explain the mechanism of exercise intolerance.
- Justify why sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise is now first-line care rather than prolonged rest, citing the evidence base that drove the paradigm shift.
- Locate the exercise professional clearly within the multidisciplinary concussion team as the implementer of graded exertion, not the diagnostician or the clearer.

### Module 2 — Recognition, Red Flags & Scope of Practice
- Identify the observable signs and reported symptom clusters of concussion, and explain the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each cluster.
- List the red flags that mandate urgent emergency escalation, and describe the pathophysiological mechanism — intracranial haemorrhage, second-impact syndrome and cervical spine injury — that makes each a medical emergency requiring immediate emergency services activation.
- Articulate the scope boundary in concussion with its medico-legal rationale: what the exercise professional can and cannot do, and why diagnosis and medical clearance require medical authority.
- Describe how SCAT6 and SCOAT6 function as serial outcome measures within scope, and identify the critical limits of these tools that prevent them from being used for diagnosis or clearance by a non-medical practitioner.
- Navigate referral-in and referral-out pathways: describe how a concussion case appropriately reaches the exercise professional, recognise when symptoms warrant routing to vestibular physiotherapy, a sports physician, psychology or neurology, and describe the practitioner's position as active-rehabilitation specialist in the multidisciplinary team.

### Module 3 — Assessment That Is the Treatment (BCTT/BCBT)
- Explain the exercise-physiology rationale for a graded exertion protocol in concussion — specifically why a near-linear cardiovascular ramp identifies the autonomic and cerebrovascular ceiling that characterises the physiological phenotype.
- Identify all pre-test contraindications and red flags, categorise them as absolute or relative, and describe the correct response to each.
- Administer the standardised Balke-based Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), including the precise per-minute incline and speed increments, continuous heart rate/RPE/symptom monitoring, and the two distinct test-termination criteria.
- Record the heart rate at symptom threshold (HRt), describe what it represents physiologically (the point where impaired cerebral autoregulation and blunted cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity can no longer meet exertional demand), and distinguish it from the voluntary-exhaustion endpoint.
- Select and administer the Buffalo Concussion Bike Test (BCBT) when the treadmill protocol is contraindicated or clinically suboptimal, and describe the validated protocol steps.
- Convert the HRt into an individualised sub-symptom-threshold aerobic prescription using the 80–90% rule, including frequency, duration and mode.
- Apply a structured re-testing schedule to progress the symptom-threshold ceiling, identify the physiological-recovery endpoint, and communicate findings to the referring clinician within scope.
- Use VOMS, BESS and timed tandem gait as serial adjunct outcome measures to track vestibular, ocular and postural-control recovery — distinguishing their role from diagnostic assessment.

### Module 4 — Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Rehabilitation
- Explain the full biochemical and physiological rationale for sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise — covering autonomic restoration, cerebral autoregulation and CO₂ reactivity normalisation, exercise-induced BDNF upregulation and neuroplasticity, neuroinflammation reduction, and systemic deconditioning reversal.
- Describe the heart-rate threshold (HRt) as the physiological marker of the autonomic/cerebrovascular ceiling, and explain why training at 80–90% of HRt (rather than at or above it) produces adaptive recovery.
- Derive a complete FITT prescription from a BCTT HRt result, including worked calculations of the target HR band.
- Apply the real-time symptom-monitoring rule (≥2-point rise = stop/reduce), distinguishing acceptable baseline noise from genuine threshold-crossing.
- Progress the programme safely using the re-test-driven progression algorithm — stepping the HR ceiling as tolerance recovers, and distinguishing the "duration first, then intensity" sequencing rule.
- Select appropriate modalities for the deconditioned, dizzy, anxious or otherwise complex patient, and adapt the prescription to the presentation.
- Recognise red flags that require regression, pause or urgent escalation, and communicate these to the referrer within scope.

### Module 5 — Phenotype-Specific Exercise Rehabilitation
- Describe the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each of the seven concussion phenotypes (vestibular, oculomotor, cervicogenic, autonomic/physiologic, cognitive/fatigue, affective, headache/migraine) and connect each mechanism to its clinical presentation.
- Prescribe, dose and progress the in-scope exercise rehabilitation for each phenotype — including VOR×1/VOR×2 gaze stabilisation, habituation and dynamic balance for the vestibular phenotype; convergence, saccade, pursuit and accommodative facility drills for the oculomotor phenotype; cranio-cervical flexion, joint-position-error retraining and postural endurance work for the cervicogenic phenotype; and sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise for the autonomic phenotype.
- Explain the cerebellar neuroplasticity mechanism by which VOR gaze-stabilisation exercises restore vestibular function, and distinguish central vestibular adaptation from peripheral otoconia repositioning.
- Describe the cervico-ocular reflex and explain how cervical proprioceptive injury contributes to dizziness and gaze instability after concussion.
- Apply symptom-titrated dosing and a three-phase progression framework across all phenotypes, including the criteria for advancing, regressing and referring.
- Identify the dominant phenotype driver when multiple phenotypes co-present, sequence the treatment plan accordingly, and layer secondary phenotype exercises appropriately.
- Recognise the presentations — BPPV, manifest tropia, clinical depression, vertebrobasilar red flags — that require referral before or instead of exercise, and identify the appropriate specialist for each.

### Module 6 — Graded Return to Activity, Sport & Performance
- Apply the international graded return-to-sport (GRTS) strategy as a staged, symptom-monitored progression — with particular depth on stages 3 and 4 and what distinguishes each from the preceding stage.
- Comply with mandated stand-down requirements and correctly apply both minimums (calendar stand-down and symptom-free period) to a real case, identifying which governs.
- Explain the physiology of reconditioning — why aerobic base must precede sport-specific load, and the specific mechanisms by which running, multidirectional movement and contact-preparation each introduce qualitatively new physiological demands.
- Design a concussion-specific return-to-running progression and team-sport conditioning programme, applying objective progress, regress and readiness criteria governed by the 24-hour symptom rule.
- Coordinate physical and cognitive load demands (return-to-learn and return-to-work pacing) and communicate an athlete's reconditioning readiness to the medical practitioner responsible for contact clearance.

### Module 7 — Persistent Symptoms & the Complex Case
- Define persistent post-concussion symptoms (PPCS) using the current consensus timeframe and terminology, and explain why PPCS is a heterogeneous clinical picture rather than a single diagnosis.
- Describe the five pathophysiological mechanisms that drive persistence beyond the acute phase: sustained autonomic and cerebrovascular dysregulation, unresolved neuroinflammation, central sensitisation, the deconditioning spiral, and psychological maintaining factors including fear-avoidance and catastrophising.
- Identify the clinical and demographic risk factors that predict prolonged recovery, and explain why early activity avoidance is itself a modifiable, iatrogenic risk factor.
- Justify, from the evidence base, why graded sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise remains indicated — and actively therapeutic — in PPCS when a genuine symptom-limited threshold is confirmed.
- Apply a four-step structured decision framework when a rehabilitation programme stalls: reassess the BCTT threshold, screen for non-aerobic drivers, re-refer with data, and escalate red flags immediately.
- Adapt rehabilitation for the comorbid, deconditioned and anxious patient using pacing, graded exposure and behaviour-change strategies within scope.
- Recognise presentations that require referral beyond the exercise-professional lane — including progressive presentations suggesting neurodegenerative concern — and describe what an accurate, in-scope response looks like in practice.

### Module 8 — Documentation, Communication & Referral
- Produce the four core in-scope concussion-rehabilitation documents: assessment/BCTT record, session and progression tracking sheet, practitioner-to-referrer communication letter, and return-to-activity progress note — each as a real, structured artifact with scope-appropriate language.
- Apply the record-within-scope principle: frame findings as exercise-physiology observations and recommendations, never as a diagnosis or medical clearance for return to contact.
- Explain the three jobs every clinical record performs simultaneously — clinical continuity, inter-professional communication, and medico-legal accountability — and recognise why all three apply to concussion records.
- Apply the privacy principles governing the health information collected, stored and disclosed in the course of concussion rehabilitation, including the obligations triggered by AI scribe tools.
- Use AI-assisted documentation tools as an efficiency aid while retaining full personal professional responsibility for the accuracy and scope-appropriateness of every clinical note filed.

## 7. Mapping to CIMSPA professional standards

The programme is submitted as **specialist post-qualification CPD**, not as a route to a core CIMSPA occupational standard. It builds on competence that endorsed practitioners already hold and extends it to a population they are not currently trained for.

**Exercise Referral Practitioner.** The closest fit. The standard requires the practitioner to receive a referral from a healthcare professional, screen for contraindications, design and deliver a safe individualised exercise programme for a person with a defined condition, monitor response, maintain records and report back to the referrer. Concussion Rehab Mastery instantiates every one of those obligations for a condition the standard does not currently name. Module 2 covers referral-in and red-flag triage; Module 3 covers pre-exercise contraindication screening and objective assessment; Modules 4–5 cover individualised programme design; Modules 6–7 cover monitoring, progression and regression; Module 8 covers record-keeping and reporting back to the referring clinician.

**Advanced Personal Trainer / Personal Trainer.** Maps to programme design and adaptation for clients with additional needs, exercise prescription from objective measures, ongoing client monitoring, and — critically — the professional-practice requirement to recognise the limits of the role and refer on. The scope-boundary content is the single most transferable element of the programme for this population.

**Strength and Conditioning Practitioner / Coach.** Maps to athlete monitoring, load management and periodisation, return-to-training reconditioning, and duty of care within a multidisciplinary performance and medical team. Module 6 is written directly to this standard: staged return-to-sport progression, load monitoring using sRPE and acute:chronic workload ratio, the 24-hour symptom rule, and handover of a documented, prepared athlete to the medical practitioner who holds the contact-clearance decision.

**Professional practice, common to all.** Ethical practice, working within the limits of competence, safeguarding, escalation, record-keeping and inter-professional communication are assessed throughout and are the explicit subject of Modules 2 and 8.

We will remap to any alternative standard the review panel prefers, and will supply a per-question mapping matrix on request.

## 8. Non-competition with CIMSPA provision

Concussion Rehab Mastery does not duplicate or compete with any CIMSPA qualification, standard or endorsed product. No CIMSPA occupational standard covers concussion rehabilitation; no endorsed product currently offered addresses heart-rate-threshold-guided post-concussion exercise prescription. The programme is complementary specialist CPD that extends the reach of existing endorsed practitioners into a condition their employers and clients increasingly expect them to handle.

## 9. UK contextualisation

The clinical protocol at the heart of this programme is international. The BCTT, the heart-rate threshold, the 80–90% prescription band, the symptom-exacerbation stop rule and the graded return-to-sport strategy are drawn from the international consensus statement and the underlying Buffalo research programme, and are applied identically in the UK.

Where jurisdiction matters — statutory return-to-play stand-down periods, data-protection obligations and the boundary of medical clearance — UK learners are directed to the UK instruments that govern their practice: the UK Government/DCMS *UK Concussion Guidelines for Non-Elite (Grassroots) Sport*, the applicable national governing body's return-to-play protocol, and UK data-protection law for clinical record-keeping. The competency being assessed — apply the mandated stand-down correctly, identify which minimum governs, document within scope and hand the clearance decision to the responsible medical practitioner — is jurisdiction-neutral, and CEA will supply a UK annex mapping each of these to the corresponding module before launch to UK members.
