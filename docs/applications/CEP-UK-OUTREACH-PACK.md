## THE SPINE — why this course exists, written for a CEP audience

For two decades, concussion management meant rest until symptoms cleared. The evidence overturned that.

The 6th International Conference on Concussion in Sport (Amsterdam, October 2022; Patricios et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 57(11), 695–711) concluded that **sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise, prescribed early from an individually derived heart-rate threshold, is first-line treatment for concussion** — not a fallback after rest fails. The threshold is derived by graded exercise testing: the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test, developed and validated over fifteen years by Leddy, Willer and colleagues at the University at Buffalo.

The effect size is not marginal. Prescribed individualised exercise improves recovery by approximately **4.6 days on average and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms** — the outcome that drives the entire downstream cost of this injury.

Read that as a clinical exercise physiologist and the implication is immediate. Concussion moved from a *rest* condition to an **exercise-prescription condition, gated on exercise testing and an individually derived heart-rate threshold.** Symptom-limited graded exercise testing. An individualised target heart-rate band. Real-time symptom monitoring against an objective stop rule. Progression by retest rather than by calendar. That is not adjacent to clinical exercise physiology — that *is* clinical exercise physiology, applied to a condition that was outside the profession's remit until 2022.

**And every relevant profession was trained before that shift.**

The clearest available evidence for this is a survey of **1,272 physiotherapists**: 70% already held concussion training, and the cohort averaged **13 out of 15 on a concussion knowledge measure.** These are not under-trained practitioners. But the same survey found specific gaps — in **confidence around return-to-play decisions**, and in **knowing when graded exertional training is actually indicated.**

That is the whole problem in one finding. The knowledge is high. The knowledge is of the previous model. Practitioners trained before Amsterdam 2022 hold a coherent, well-retained, thoroughly obsolete framework — and the exact thing they are uncertain about is the exact thing the new model turns on.

**Concussion Rehab Mastery is the update, not the remedial.** It assumes the learner already holds exercise physiology competence — cardiorespiratory response to exercise, graded exercise testing principles, heart-rate and RPE monitoring, FITT programming. It does not teach those. It teaches the concussion-specific protocol, the phenotype reasoning and the scope boundary that sit on top of them. A registrant coming to this course is not filling a hole in their education. They are updating a currency that the literature moved out from under them in October 2022.
