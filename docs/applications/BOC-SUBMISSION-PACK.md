## 5. APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS — mapped to what we submit

### 5.1 Standard 1 "Evidence" — the three items that now carry non-compliance weight

**A. Practice Gap** ("what is it ATs do not know or are not doing?") — submit:

> Among 1,307 certified Athletic Trainers surveyed in the profession's own journal, only 52.7% used the recommended 3-domain minimum concussion-assessment battery, only 57.3% used the current SCAT instrument, and 8.0% still applied severity-grading scales abandoned by international consensus (Lempke, Schmidt & Lynall, *J Athl Train* 2020;55(1):17–26). Concurrently, the 6th International Consensus (Amsterdam 2022; Patricios et al. 2023) established early sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise as first-line treatment — a paradigm most practising clinicians were never trained on: the pivotal RCT (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics* 2019) shortened median recovery from 17 to 13 days. ATs hold return-to-play involvement in 93.8% of cases yet assessment practice and treatment currency measurably lag the evidence.

**B. Measurable Learning Objectives** — 3 to 5, Bloom's, "M.O.V." (measurable, observable, visual). **BANNED verbs: learn, understand, know.** For the free course (US edition):

1. Identify the red-flag findings in a suspected concussion that require immediate emergency activation.
2. Differentiate current evidence-based management (24–48 h relative rest, then sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise) from the superseded strict-rest model.
3. Select the appropriate recognition instrument (SCAT6 vs SCOAT6) based on time since injury.
4. Apply the recognise–remove–refer sequence to a sideline scenario, including the no-same-day-return-to-play rule.
5. Match referral urgency (emergency / urgent within 24 hours / routine review) to a given clinical presentation.

> NOTE: the existing 104 certificate's fifth learning outcome begins "Understand which recognition tool…" — **reword to "Select…" before submission** (build item B3).

**C. Clinical Bottom Line** — 3–4 sentences identifying the clinical recommendation:

> Following a brief 24–48-hour period of relative rest, early sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise is first-line treatment for concussion and shortens recovery (median 13 vs 17 days; Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics* 2019). Prolonged strict rest is associated with slower recovery and is no longer recommended (Patricios et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2023). An athlete with suspected concussion must be removed from play immediately with no same-day return, screened against red flags, and referred at an urgency matching the presentation. Recognition uses SCAT6 within 72 hours of injury and SCOAT6 thereafter.
