## READ THIS SECOND — the mechanics, which are specific and non-obvious

**HPCSA does not accredit providers. There is no provider scheme to join.**

HPCSA maintains a register of **approved accreditors**. Each accreditor assesses activities submitted to it and, on approval, issues an **accreditation number and an accreditation letter**. Practitioners then claim CEUs against that number.

The approved accreditors relevant here:

- **UCT** — University of Cape Town, an approved accreditor with a published fee schedule and a documented application route.
- **SAMA** — South African Medical Association.
- **SAAFP** — South African Academy of Family Physicians.

## READ THIS THIRD — two facts that make this viable, and one that makes it attractive

### Non-resident and overseas-online is explicitly fine

This is the question that stops most overseas providers, and the answer is favourable.

The HPCSA CPD guidelines recognise **activities attended abroad**, and set out the principle that a South African accreditor **should accredit an international activity where that activity is not already accredited or recognised locally.** The guidelines contemplate exactly this situation: a South African practitioner encounters valuable education originating outside South Africa, and the accreditation system exists to bring it into the CEU framework rather than to exclude it.

An overseas-authored, wholly online course **is accreditable.** CEA being Australian is not a bar. CRM being asynchronous and self-paced is not a bar. What matters to the accreditor is the standard of the activity, the assessment, the evidence base and the relevance to the practitioner's scope — all of which are documented below.

There is also no local substitute to displace it. There is, to CEA's knowledge, no South African-accredited CPD activity covering post-concussion exercise prescription to this depth — which is the precise circumstance the guideline principle addresses.

### One accreditation reaches more than one profession

Once an activity is accredited for one professional board, **any HPCSA-registered practitioner may attend it and claim the CEUs, provided the activity is relevant to their own scope of practice.**

That is a significant multiplier and it should shape how the activity is framed in the submission. A single accreditation obtained through the biokinetics route is claimable by **physiotherapists, and by other HPCSA-registered practitioners whose scope includes exercise-based rehabilitation** — not only biokineticists.

The submission below is therefore written to **name the multi-professional relevance explicitly**, describe the target audience in scope terms rather than in single-profession terms, and make the relevance to each group visible on the face of the application. This is not padding. It is what allows one R1,380 fee to serve several professions.

### Why biokinetics is the right front door

**Biokineticist scope is, in its statutory formulation, individualised assessment and exercise prescription for clinical pathology.** Concussion Rehab Mastery sits dead centre of that. It is not adjacent to biokinetics practice; it is a condition-specific application of the exact competence that defines the profession — symptom-limited graded exercise testing, derivation of an individual heart-rate threshold, prescription against that threshold, monitored progression by objective criteria.

Two further factors:

- **HPCSA CEUs are statutory.** CPD is not optional professional garnish in South Africa; it is a condition of continued registration. That makes accredited activity a genuine purchase rather than a discretionary one.
- **Rugby volume.** South Africa carries one of the world's highest per-capita burdens of sport-related concussion, concentrated in a sport where return-to-play decisions are visible, contested and consequential. Demand for competent post-concussion exercise rehabilitation is not hypothetical there.

## THE SPINE — the case for the activity

For two decades, concussion management meant rest until symptoms cleared. The evidence overturned that.

The 6th International Conference on Concussion in Sport (Amsterdam, October 2022; Patricios et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 57(11), 695–711) concluded that **sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise, prescribed early from an individually derived heart-rate threshold, is first-line treatment for concussion** — not a fallback after rest has failed. The threshold is derived by graded exercise testing: the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test, developed and validated over fifteen years by Leddy, Willer and colleagues at the University at Buffalo, whose randomised trials in *JAMA Pediatrics* (2019) and *Lancet Child & Adolescent Health* (2021) established that early prescribed aerobic exercise shortens recovery.

The magnitude matters. Prescribed individualised exercise improves recovery by approximately **4.6 days on average and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms** — persisting symptoms being the outcome that drives almost all of the long-term cost and disability attached to this injury.

That decision moved the central treatment of concussion into the hands of the practitioner whose defining competence is individualised exercise prescription for clinical pathology. In South Africa, that is the biokineticist.

**But every relevant profession was trained before that shift.**

The clearest evidence for this is a survey of **1,272 physiotherapists**. Seventy percent already held concussion training. The cohort averaged **13 out of 15** on a concussion knowledge measure. These are not under-trained practitioners. Yet the same survey identified specific gaps: **confidence in return-to-play decision-making**, and **knowing when graded exertional training is actually indicated.**

That finding is the whole justification for this activity. Knowledge is high. The knowledge is of the previous model. Practitioners trained before October 2022 hold a coherent, well-retained framework built on rest — and the two things they are least sure of are the two things the current model turns on.

**Concussion Rehab Mastery is positioned as the update to the current model, not as remedial education.** It assumes and does not teach undergraduate-level exercise physiology — cardiorespiratory response to exercise, graded exercise test principles, heart-rate and RPE monitoring, FITT programming. What it adds is the concussion-specific protocol, the phenotype reasoning, and the scope boundary that governs the practitioner's part of the pathway.

## 3. Activity description

**Concussion Rehab Mastery** trains practitioners to deliver the exercise-based treatment of concussion that international consensus now places first-line.

The 6th International Conference on Concussion in Sport (Amsterdam, October 2022; Patricios et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 57(11), 695–711) concluded that sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise, prescribed from a heart-rate threshold derived by a graded exercise test, is first-line treatment for concussion. The test that produces that threshold is the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), developed and validated over fifteen years at the University at Buffalo. Randomised trials from that group established that early prescribed aerobic exercise shortens recovery — by approximately 4.6 days on average — and reduces the incidence of persisting post-concussion symptoms.

Deriving an individualised heart-rate target from a symptom-limited graded exercise test, prescribing a dose against it, monitoring symptom response in real time and progressing load against objective criteria is core exercise-rehabilitation practice. What is missing from most practitioners' training is the concussion-specific protocol, the phenotype reasoning, and a clear account of where the practitioner's lane ends.

The activity delivers all three across eight modules: the pathophysiology that explains why exercise works and why it must stay below threshold; recognition and red-flag triage; the complete BCTT and Buffalo Concussion Bike Test protocols; conversion of the heart-rate threshold into a full FITT prescription; phenotype-specific rehabilitation across the seven recognised concussion phenotypes; the six-stage graded return-to-sport strategy with load monitoring and reconditioning physiology; persistent post-concussion symptoms and the programme that will not progress; and the documentation, privacy obligations and inter-professional communication that close the loop with the referring clinician.

**Scope discipline runs through every module.** Diagnosis of concussion is a medical act. Clearance for return to contact is a medical act. The practitioner trained by this activity implements graded exertion, measures it, documents it, escalates red flags, and returns a prepared athlete to the practitioner who holds the clearance decision. Learners are assessed on that boundary as rigorously as on the protocol.

## 4. Relevance to practitioner scope — multi-professional

**Biokineticists.** The scope of biokinetics is individualised assessment and exercise prescription for clinical pathology. **The gap this addresses is one of currency, not of competence.** A survey of 1,272 physiotherapists found 70% already held concussion training, with the cohort averaging 13/15 on knowledge — but with specific deficits in return-to-play confidence and in knowing when graded exertional training is indicated. The profession is well trained on a model that international consensus superseded in October 2022. This activity is the update.

## 7. Assessment

Assessment is **distributed and per-module**, not a single terminal examination.

- **87 assessment questions**, allocated across the eight modules in proportion to weight.
- Questions are delivered **within each module**, at the point of learning, so a learner cannot advance past a protocol step they have not understood.
- **80% pass mark at module level.**
- Items test protocol accuracy (per-minute BCTT increments, the two termination criteria), physiological reasoning (why the symptom threshold represents an autonomic and cerebrovascular ceiling), clinical decision-making (progress, regress, refer) and scope discipline, including red-flag escalation and the boundary at return-to-contact clearance.

- Certification requires every module at or above the pass mark.

## 8. Learning outcomes

The activity carries **51 assessed learning objectives**, reproduced in full below as delivered.

### Module 1 — Concussion for the Exercise Physiologist
- Define concussion as a functional brain injury, distinguish it from structural traumatic brain injury, and explain why standard neuroimaging is typically normal.
- Describe the biomechanical mechanisms of concussion — rotational, linear, coup-contrecoup and diffuse axonal injury — and explain why rotational forces are considered most damaging.
- Trace the neurometabolic cascade from mechanoporation through ionic flux, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase overdrive, hyperglycolysis, Ca²⁺-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation to recovery — and connect each phase to clinical symptoms.
- Explain cerebral blood-flow dysregulation after concussion, including impaired autoregulation, neurovascular uncoupling and reduced cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity, and describe how each contributes to symptom provocation during exertion.
- Describe post-concussion autonomic dysfunction — sympathovagal imbalance, blunted baroreflex sensitivity and reduced HRV — and explain the mechanism of exercise intolerance.
- Justify why sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise is now first-line care rather than prolonged rest, citing the evidence base that drove the paradigm shift.
- Locate the exercise practitioner clearly within the multidisciplinary concussion team as the implementer of graded exertion, not the diagnostician or the clearer.

### Module 2 — Recognition, Red Flags & Scope of Practice
- Identify the observable signs and reported symptom clusters of concussion, and explain the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each cluster.
- List the red flags that mandate urgent emergency escalation, and describe the pathophysiological mechanism — intracranial haemorrhage, second-impact syndrome and cervical spine injury — that makes each a medical emergency requiring immediate emergency services activation.
- Articulate the scope boundary in concussion with its medico-legal rationale: what the exercise practitioner can and cannot do, and why diagnosis and medical clearance require medical authority.
- Describe how SCAT6 and SCOAT6 function as serial outcome measures within scope, and identify the critical limits of these tools that prevent them from being used for diagnosis or clearance by a non-medical practitioner.
- Navigate referral-in and referral-out pathways: describe how a concussion case appropriately reaches the exercise practitioner, recognise when symptoms warrant routing to vestibular physiotherapy, a sports physician, psychology or neurology, and describe the practitioner's position as active-rehabilitation specialist in the multidisciplinary team.

### Module 3 — Assessment That Is the Treatment (BCTT/BCBT)
- Explain the exercise-physiology rationale for a graded exertion protocol in concussion — specifically why a near-linear cardiovascular ramp identifies the autonomic and cerebrovascular ceiling that characterises the physiological phenotype.
- Identify all pre-test contraindications and red flags, categorise them as absolute or relative, and describe the correct response to each.
- Administer the standardised Balke-based Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), including the precise per-minute incline and speed increments, continuous heart rate/RPE/symptom monitoring, and the two distinct test-termination criteria.
- Record the heart rate at symptom threshold (HRt), describe what it represents physiologically (the point where impaired cerebral autoregulation and blunted cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity can no longer meet exertional demand), and distinguish it from the voluntary-exhaustion endpoint.
- Select and administer the Buffalo Concussion Bike Test (BCBT) when the treadmill protocol is contraindicated or clinically suboptimal, and describe the validated protocol steps.
- Convert the HRt into an individualised sub-symptom-threshold aerobic prescription using the 80–90% rule, including frequency, duration and mode.
- Apply a structured re-testing schedule to progress the symptom-threshold ceiling, identify the physiological-recovery endpoint, and communicate findings to the referring clinician within scope.
- Use VOMS, BESS and timed tandem gait as serial adjunct outcome measures to track vestibular, ocular and postural-control recovery — distinguishing their role from diagnostic assessment.

### Module 4 — Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Rehabilitation
- Explain the full biochemical and physiological rationale for sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise — covering autonomic restoration, cerebral autoregulation and CO₂ reactivity normalisation, exercise-induced BDNF upregulation and neuroplasticity, neuroinflammation reduction, and systemic deconditioning reversal.
- Describe the heart-rate threshold (HRt) as the physiological marker of the autonomic/cerebrovascular ceiling, and explain why training at 80–90% of HRt (rather than at or above it) produces adaptive recovery.
- Derive a complete FITT prescription from a BCTT HRt result, including worked calculations of the target HR band.
- Apply the real-time symptom-monitoring rule (≥2-point rise = stop/reduce), distinguishing acceptable baseline noise from genuine threshold-crossing.
- Progress the programme safely using the re-test-driven progression algorithm — stepping the HR ceiling as tolerance recovers, and distinguishing the "duration first, then intensity" sequencing rule.
- Select appropriate modalities for the deconditioned, dizzy, anxious or otherwise complex patient, and adapt the prescription to the presentation.
- Recognise red flags that require regression, pause or urgent escalation, and communicate these to the referrer within scope.

### Module 5 — Phenotype-Specific Exercise Rehabilitation
- Describe the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each of the seven concussion phenotypes (vestibular, oculomotor, cervicogenic, autonomic/physiologic, cognitive/fatigue, affective, headache/migraine) and connect each mechanism to its clinical presentation.
- Prescribe, dose and progress the in-scope exercise rehabilitation for each phenotype — including VOR×1/VOR×2 gaze stabilisation, habituation and dynamic balance for the vestibular phenotype; convergence, saccade, pursuit and accommodative facility drills for the oculomotor phenotype; cranio-cervical flexion, joint-position-error retraining and postural endurance work for the cervicogenic phenotype; and sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise for the autonomic phenotype.
- Explain the cerebellar neuroplasticity mechanism by which VOR gaze-stabilisation exercises restore vestibular function, and distinguish central vestibular adaptation from peripheral otoconia repositioning.
- Describe the cervico-ocular reflex and explain how cervical proprioceptive injury contributes to dizziness and gaze instability after concussion.
- Apply symptom-titrated dosing and a three-phase progression framework across all phenotypes, including the criteria for advancing, regressing and referring.
- Identify the dominant phenotype driver when multiple phenotypes co-present, sequence the treatment plan accordingly, and layer secondary phenotype exercises appropriately.
- Recognise the presentations — BPPV, manifest tropia, clinical depression, vertebrobasilar red flags — that require referral before or instead of exercise, and identify the appropriate specialist for each.

### Module 6 — Graded Return to Activity, Sport & Performance
- Apply the international graded return-to-sport (GRTS) strategy as a staged, symptom-monitored progression — with particular depth on stages 3 and 4 and what distinguishes each from the preceding stage.
- Comply with mandated stand-down requirements and correctly apply both minimums (calendar stand-down and symptom-free period) to a real case, identifying which governs.
- Explain the physiology of reconditioning — why aerobic base must precede sport-specific load, and the specific mechanisms by which running, multidirectional movement and contact-preparation each introduce qualitatively new physiological demands.
- Design a concussion-specific return-to-running progression and team-sport conditioning programme, applying objective progress, regress and readiness criteria governed by the 24-hour symptom rule.
- Coordinate physical and cognitive load demands (return-to-learn and return-to-work pacing) and communicate an athlete's reconditioning readiness to the medical practitioner responsible for contact clearance.

### Module 7 — Persistent Symptoms & the Complex Case
- Define persistent post-concussion symptoms (PPCS) using the current consensus timeframe and terminology, and explain why PPCS is a heterogeneous clinical picture rather than a single diagnosis.
- Describe the five pathophysiological mechanisms that drive persistence beyond the acute phase: sustained autonomic and cerebrovascular dysregulation, unresolved neuroinflammation, central sensitisation, the deconditioning spiral, and psychological maintaining factors including fear-avoidance and catastrophising.
- Identify the clinical and demographic risk factors that predict prolonged recovery, and explain why early activity avoidance is itself a modifiable, iatrogenic risk factor.
- Justify, from the evidence base, why graded sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise remains indicated — and actively therapeutic — in PPCS when a genuine symptom-limited threshold is confirmed.
- Apply a four-step structured decision framework when a rehabilitation programme stalls: reassess the BCTT threshold, screen for non-aerobic drivers, re-refer with data, and escalate red flags immediately.
- Adapt rehabilitation for the comorbid, deconditioned and anxious patient using pacing, graded exposure and behaviour-change strategies within scope.
- Recognise presentations that require referral beyond the exercise-practitioner lane — including progressive presentations suggesting neurodegenerative concern — and describe what an accurate, in-scope response looks like in practice.

### Module 8 — Documentation, Communication & Referral
- Produce the four core in-scope concussion-rehabilitation documents: assessment/BCTT record, session and progression tracking sheet, practitioner-to-referrer communication letter, and return-to-activity progress note — each as a real, structured artifact with scope-appropriate language.
- Apply the record-within-scope principle: frame findings as exercise-physiology observations and recommendations, never as a diagnosis or medical clearance for return to contact.
- Explain the three jobs every clinical record performs simultaneously — clinical continuity, inter-professional communication, and medico-legal accountability — and recognise why all three apply to concussion records.
- Apply the privacy principles governing the health information collected, stored and disclosed in the course of concussion rehabilitation, including the obligations triggered by AI scribe tools.
- Use AI-assisted documentation tools as an efficiency aid while retaining full personal professional responsibility for the accuracy and scope-appropriateness of every clinical note filed.

## 9. Applicability in the South African setting

**The clinical protocol is international, not jurisdictional.** The BCTT, the heart-rate threshold, the 80–90% prescription band, the ≥2-point symptom-exacerbation stop rule, the retest-driven progression algorithm, the seven-phenotype framework and the six-stage graded return-to-sport strategy all derive from the international consensus statement and the peer-reviewed literature behind it. They are applied identically in Cape Town and in Melbourne. Nothing in the physiological content of Modules 1, 3, 4, 5 or 7 is Australia-specific.

**Return-to-play governance.** Module 6 teaches the transferable competency: identify the two governing minimums (a mandated calendar stand-down and a symptom-free period), determine which one governs in a given case, and refuse to progress an athlete past the stage the applicable governance permits. The Australian instrument in the core content is presented as a worked example, not as a governing rule. CEA supplies a **South African annex** setting out the applicable South African instruments — including World Rugby's graduated return-to-play framework as applied by SA Rugby, and the relevant national sporting-body protocols — alongside the module content, so the South African learner exercises the competency against the rules that bind them. [ZAC: this annex must be written and must exist as a document before the submission is sent; it is offered to the assessor as part of the application.]

**Scope of practice.** The boundary the activity teaches maps directly onto HPCSA practice: diagnosis of concussion and clearance for return to contact are medical acts. Biokineticists, physiotherapists and other HPCSA-registered practitioners in this pathway operate under a referral relationship with a medical practitioner and none of them clears an athlete for contact. Module 2 teaches recognise-and-escalate rather than diagnose. Module 8 teaches the practitioner to record findings as exercise-rehabilitation observations and recommendations, never as diagnosis or clearance. The material states explicitly that the learner's own regulator and professional board define their lane, and directs learners to it.

## 11. CEU request and rationale

**Requested: 8 clinical CEUs, plus 1 ethics CEU.**

### Clinical CEUs — 8

The request is built on the most conservative possible basis: **one CEU per hour of assessed instructional content, and nothing else.**

- Total instructional content: **480 minutes = 8.0 hours.**
- Hours counted: **assessed instructional content only.** No hours are claimed for reading, reflection, optional material, navigation, discussion or the production of portfolio evidence — all of which practitioners will in fact spend time on, and none of which is included in this figure.
- **Every hour claimed is assessed.** The activity carries 87 questions delivered within the modules at the point of learning, with an 80% pass mark applied at module level and certification withheld until every module meets it. There is no unassessed hour in the 8.0.
- The per-module distribution of hours is set out in §5 and can be verified against the platform's per-module progress data.

**Why the assessment structure matters to the CEU calculation.** Distributed per-module assessment with a written rationale returned on every answer is a materially stronger verification of learning than a single terminal quiz taken after the fact. A learner cannot pass Module 3 without demonstrating the BCTT protocol correctly, and cannot reach Module 4's prescription content having failed it. The CEUs claimed correspond to hours in which competence was demonstrated, not hours in which content was displayed.

### Ethics CEU — 1

**Module 8 — Documentation, Communication & Referral (30 min)** — together with the scope-of-practice content in **Module 2 (30 min)** — comprises 60 minutes addressing ethics, human rights and health law as those apply to this clinical pathway:
