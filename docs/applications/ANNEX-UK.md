## 2. UK professional context — exercise-based concussion rehabilitation

Amsterdam 2022 (Patricios et al., *BJSM*, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106898) moved concussion from a rest condition to an exercise-prescription condition, gated on graded exercise testing and an individually derived heart-rate threshold. Early prescribed sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise shortened median recovery from 17 days to 13 days in the pivotal randomised trial (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics*, 2019, https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2018.4397; n=103, P=.009) and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms (Leddy et al., *Lancet Child Adolesc Health*, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(21)00267-4; Leddy et al., *BJSM* meta-analysis, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106676).

In UK performance and clinical settings the person running that graded exertion is very often the sport and exercise scientist, the clinical exercise physiologist, or the S&C coach — they hold the exercise-testing and heart-rate-prescription competency but were, like every relevant profession, trained on the pre-2022 model. CRM supplies the concussion-specific protocol layered on top of a competency the UK practitioner already holds. It is the update to the model that moved, not remedial education.

The method itself is published open-access and citable, so a UK reviewer can inspect the standard the course teaches rather than take it on the provider's word: *A Standardised Clinical Protocol for Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Exercise Rehabilitation after Concussion (mild Traumatic Brain Injury)* (Lewis, Z., 2026; Zenodo; open access, CC-BY-4.0; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21482634). The SST Trainer bundled with CRM is the tool a UK practitioner uses to deliver that protocol in practice — running the graded exercise test, capturing the **measured** heart-rate threshold, issuing and monitoring the sub-symptom-threshold prescription, and producing a measured HR-threshold trajectory with standardised progress reporting (demo: `/sst-trainer?clinic=DEMO00`).

**Mapping into the verified CEP-UK / AHCS registration framework.** Registration as a Clinical Exercise Physiologist requires proficiency across **six areas of practice for nine health conditions** (source: CEP-UK, verified in `CEP-UK-FRAMEWORK-VERIFIED.md`). Concussion/mTBI is **not a named exemplar** in the framework; it maps **by clinical extension into the Neurological/Neuromuscular condition** (the framework's named neuro exemplars are stroke, dementia, Parkinson's, multiple sclerosis). CRM is presented as evidence *within* the Neurological/Neuromuscular condition, across the six practice areas:

## 4. Scope-of-practice fit for UK exercise professionals

In the UK, as in Australia, **diagnosis of concussion and medical clearance for return to contact are medical acts.** CRM teaches recognise-and-escalate (Module 2), never diagnose; and to record findings as exercise-physiology observations and recommendations, never as diagnosis or clearance (Module 8). That boundary maps cleanly onto every UK target audience, each of whom operates under a referral-in relationship with a medical practitioner and none of whom clears an athlete for contact:

- **CASES Accredited Sport and Exercise Scientists** (and those on the Chartered Scientist / CSci route).
- **Clinical exercise physiologists** registered with the **Academy for Healthcare Science (AHCS)** via the CEP-UK register (front-of-house: clinicalexercisephysiology.org.uk; register operator: ahcs.ac.uk).
- **UKSCA-accredited strength & conditioning coaches** carrying return-to-training reconditioning within a multidisciplinary team.
- **CIMSPA-registered exercise referral practitioners and advanced personal trainers** receiving healthcare referrals (CRM maps to the Exercise Referral Practitioner / Advanced PT / S&C standards).
- **HCPC-registered physiotherapists (CSP members)** with a sport and exercise medicine caseload — where their own regulator and professional standards govern their lane, and the material directs them to it.

## 5. UK return-to-play governance

Module 6 teaches the transferable competency: identify the two governing minimums (a mandated calendar stand-down and a symptom-free period), determine which governs in a given case, and refuse to progress an athlete past the stage the governance permits. UK learners exercise that competency against:

- **The UK Government / DCMS *UK Concussion Guidelines for Non-Elite (Grassroots) Sport*** as the baseline instrument.
- **The athlete's national governing body protocol** where they are under one — including the graduated return-to-play frameworks of the **RFU**, **The FA**, the **Rugby Football League**, and **World Rugby's graduated return-to-play**.

The Australian instrument cited in the delivered content is presented as the worked example, not as the governing rule for a UK practitioner. **ACTION: confirm the current published titles/versions of the DCMS grassroots guidance and each named NGB protocol on the body's own site before the formal submission goes out.**
