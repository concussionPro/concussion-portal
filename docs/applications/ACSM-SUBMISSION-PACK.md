## POSITIONING SPINE — read before editing any section

> Internal framing note. This governs the argument shape of every asset below. It is not itself pasted into a Formstack field.

**The demand reality for ACSM: this is SCOPE EXPANSION, not unmet pain.**

ACSM-EP and ACSM-CEP holders are not in distress about concussion. They are not encountering a problem they cannot solve and searching for a remedy. What they have is a mandatory **60 CECs per three-year cycle** to fill, a CEC catalogue and approved-provider list that contains **zero concussion provision**, and a competency they already hold that the 2022 evidence base has just made the gateway to a patient population they are not currently seeing.

So the offer is **a new billable patient population, accessible with a competency you already hold** — a service-line expansion — and the CEC requirement is the mechanism that makes acquiring it free at the margin. It is emphatically **not** "you have a dangerous knowledge gap." An EP or CEP who reads a deficiency pitch reads it as a stranger telling them their credential is inadequate, and disengages.

**The evidentiary frame that supports it.** Amsterdam 2022 moved concussion from a **rest** condition to an **exercise-prescription** condition, gated on exercise testing and on an individually derived heart-rate threshold. Prescribed individualised exercise shortened median recovery from 17 days to 13 days in the pivotal randomised trial (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics* 2019; n=103 adolescent athletes aged 13-18, P=.009), and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms. Every relevant profession was trained **before** that shift. A survey of **1,272 physiotherapists** found **70% already held concussion training** and averaged **13 out of 15** on concussion knowledge — with the specific gaps in return-to-play confidence and in knowing **when graded exertional training is indicated**. **The line to land: they are not ignorant — they are current on the old model.** CRM is the update to the new model. **Never** frame it as remedial education or as fixing ignorance.

**The internal-to-ACSM corroboration.** ACSM's own Hot Topic article, *Exercise & Rest in Concussion Recovery* (Hildenbrand and Herring, FACSM, 23 April 2025), names FITT-based exercise prescription for concussion as essential future work. That is ACSM stating, in its own publication, that this translation task is unfinished. It is cited throughout as a public statement of the field's needs — never as endorsement of this course.

**Consequences for the argument shape:**
- Lead with the population and the service line, not with risk or deficiency.
- The empty CEC catalogue is an **opportunity for ACSM's members**, framed as a category ACSM's own commentary has flagged, not a criticism of ACSM's provision.
- Keep the non-competition argument (A4) prominent — a category that is empty rather than contested is the cleanest possible read for the CPE Sub-committee.
- Never imply EPs or CEPs are unsafe, undertrained or behind. They hold the competency; what moved was the indication for using it.

Concussion Rehab Mastery is an eight-module online course totalling 480 minutes (8.0 contact hours) of instructional content, excluding assessment time. Modules are sequential and gated: a learner must pass the assessment attached to a module before the next module unlocks. All content is text-and-figure based, self-paced, and available on desktop and mobile.

**Module 1 — Concussion for the Exercise Physiologist (60 min).** Establishes concussion as a functional rather than structural injury and explains why standard neuroimaging is normal. Covers injury biomechanics (rotational, linear, coup-contrecoup, diffuse axonal), then traces the neurometabolic cascade from mechanoporation through ionic flux, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase overdrive, hyperglycolysis, calcium-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation. Explains cerebral blood-flow dysregulation and post-concussion autonomic dysfunction as the physiological substrate of exercise intolerance, and situates the exercise professional within the multidisciplinary team as the implementer of graded exertion. Sections: mechanism of injury; the neurometabolic cascade; the paradigm shift and the exercise professional's defined role.

**Module 2 — Recognition, Red Flags & Scope of Practice (30 min).** Observable signs and reported symptom clusters, each mapped to its neurophysiological mechanism. Red-flag pathophysiology — intracranial haemorrhage, second-impact syndrome, cervical spine injury — and the emergency escalation response. The scope boundary with its medico-legal rationale: what an exercise professional may and may not do, why diagnosis and medical clearance require medical authority. SCAT6 and SCOAT6 as serial outcome measures within scope, including the limits that prevent their use for diagnosis or clearance. Referral-in and referral-out pathways.

**Module 3 — Assessment That Is the Treatment (90 min).** The exercise-physiology rationale for graded exertion testing in concussion. Absolute and relative pre-test contraindications and the correct response to each. Full administration of the standardised Balke-based Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), including per-minute incline and speed increments, continuous heart rate, RPE and symptom monitoring, and the two distinct test-termination criteria. Identification of the heart-rate threshold (HRt) and its physiological meaning. The Buffalo Concussion Bike Test (BCBT) as the validated alternative. Conversion of HRt into an individualised prescription. Re-testing schedules, progression rules, and VOMS, BESS and timed tandem gait as serial adjunct outcome measures. Includes a worked case.

**Module 4 — Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Rehabilitation (90 min).** The five physiological mechanisms by which sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise (SSTAE) drives recovery: autonomic restoration, normalisation of cerebral autoregulation and cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity, exercise-induced BDNF upregulation and neuroplasticity, reduction of neuroinflammation, and reversal of systemic deconditioning. Derivation of a complete FITT prescription from a BCTT result with worked target-heart-rate calculations. The real-time symptom-monitoring rule. The re-test-driven progression algorithm and the duration-before-intensity sequencing rule. Interval and cross-training variants, the deconditioned and anxious patient, red flags requiring regression or escalation.

**Module 5 — Phenotype-Specific Exercise Rehabilitation (60 min).** Neurophysiology, recognition and exercise prescription across seven phenotypes (vestibular, oculomotor, cervicogenic, autonomic/physiologic, cognitive/fatigue, affective, headache/migraine). VOR×1/VOR×2 gaze stabilisation, habituation and dynamic balance work; convergence, saccade, pursuit and accommodative facility drills; cranio-cervical flexion, joint-position-error retraining and postural endurance. Cerebellar neuroplasticity as the mechanism of vestibular adaptation, and the cervico-ocular reflex. Dominant-driver identification when phenotypes overlap, a three-phase progression framework, and an integrated case. Closes with the presentations that require referral before or instead of exercise.

**Module 6 — Graded Return to Activity, Sport & Performance (60 min).** The international graded return-to-sport strategy delivered as a staged, symptom-monitored progression, with particular depth on stages 3 and 4. Jurisdictional stand-down requirements and the correct application of concurrent calendar and symptom-free minimums. The physiology of reconditioning: why aerobic base must precede sport-specific load, and the distinct demands introduced by running, multidirectional movement and contact preparation. Design of a concussion-specific return-to-running progression and team-sport conditioning program governed by the 24-hour symptom rule. Coordination of physical with cognitive load (return-to-learn, return-to-work) and scope-appropriate handover to the clinician responsible for contact clearance.

**Module 7 — Persistent Symptoms & the Complex Case (60 min).** Persistent post-concussion symptoms (PPCS): consensus definition, and the five mechanisms driving persistence — sustained autonomic and cerebrovascular dysregulation, unresolved neuroinflammation, central sensitisation, the deconditioning spiral, and psychological maintaining factors including fear-avoidance and catastrophising. Prognostic risk factors, including early activity avoidance as a modifiable iatrogenic risk. The evidence that graded SSTAE remains indicated in PPCS. A four-step decision framework for the stalled program. Pacing, graded exposure and behaviour-change strategies within scope. Presentations requiring referral beyond the exercise-professional lane.

**Module 8 — Documentation, Communication & Referral (30 min).** Production of the four core scope-appropriate documents: the assessment/BCTT record, the session and progression tracking sheet, the practitioner-to-referrer communication letter, and the return-to-activity progress note. The record-within-scope principle. The three simultaneous functions of a clinical record. Applicable privacy obligations for collected, stored and disclosed health information, including obligations triggered by AI scribe tools, and the retention of full personal professional responsibility for every note filed.

## A2 — Measurable Learning Objectives

Fifty-one measurable objectives across eight modules. Each is stated as an observable, assessable behaviour and is directly mapped to the module's question set.

### Module 1 — Concussion for the Exercise Physiologist (7 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Define concussion as a functional brain injury, distinguish it from structural traumatic brain injury, and explain why standard neuroimaging is typically normal.
2. Describe the biomechanical mechanisms of concussion — rotational, linear, coup-contrecoup and diffuse axonal injury — and explain why rotational forces are considered most damaging.
3. Trace the neurometabolic cascade from mechanoporation through ionic flux, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase overdrive, hyperglycolysis, Ca²⁺-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation to recovery — and connect each phase to clinical symptoms.
4. Explain cerebral blood-flow dysregulation after concussion, including impaired autoregulation, neurovascular uncoupling and reduced cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity, and describe how each contributes to symptom provocation during exertion.
5. Describe post-concussion autonomic dysfunction — sympathovagal imbalance, blunted baroreflex sensitivity and reduced HRV — and explain the mechanism of exercise intolerance.
6. Justify why sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise is now first-line care rather than prolonged rest, citing the evidence base that drove the paradigm shift.
7. Locate the exercise physiologist clearly within the multidisciplinary concussion team as the implementer of graded exertion, not the diagnostician or the clearer.

### Module 2 — Recognition, Red Flags & Scope of Practice (5 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Identify the observable signs and reported symptom clusters of concussion, and explain the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each cluster.
2. List the red flags that mandate urgent emergency escalation, and describe the pathophysiological mechanism — intracranial haemorrhage, second-impact syndrome and cervical spine injury — that makes each a medical emergency requiring emergency services activation.
3. Articulate the exercise physiologist's scope boundary in concussion with its medico-legal rationale: what the practitioner can and cannot do, and why diagnosis and medical clearance require medical authority.
4. Describe how SCAT6 and SCOAT6 function as serial outcome measures within exercise-physiology scope, and identify the critical limits of these tools that prevent them from being used for diagnosis or clearance.
5. Navigate referral-in and referral-out pathways: describe how a concussion case appropriately reaches the exercise physiologist, recognise when symptoms warrant routing to vestibular physiotherapy, a sports physician, psychology or neurology, and describe the practitioner's position as active-rehabilitation specialist in the multidisciplinary team.

### Module 3 — Assessment That Is the Treatment (8 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Explain the exercise-physiology rationale for a graded exertion protocol in concussion — specifically why a near-linear cardiovascular ramp identifies the autonomic and cerebrovascular ceiling that characterises the physiological phenotype.
2. Identify all pre-test contraindications and red flags, categorise them as absolute or relative, and describe the correct response to each.
3. Administer the standardised Balke-based Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), including the precise per-minute incline and speed increments, continuous heart rate/RPE/symptom monitoring, and the two distinct test-termination criteria.
4. Record the heart rate at symptom threshold (HRt), describe what it represents physiologically (the point where impaired cerebral autoregulation and blunted cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity can no longer meet exertional demand), and distinguish it from the voluntary-exhaustion endpoint.
5. Select and administer the Buffalo Concussion Bike Test (BCBT) when the treadmill protocol is contraindicated or clinically suboptimal, and describe the validated protocol steps.
6. Convert the HRt into an individualised sub-symptom-threshold aerobic prescription using the 80–90% rule, including frequency, duration and mode.
7. Apply a structured re-testing schedule to progress the symptom-threshold ceiling, identify the physiological-recovery endpoint, and communicate findings to the referring clinician within scope.
8. Use VOMS, BESS and timed tandem gait as serial adjunct outcome measures to track vestibular, ocular and postural-control recovery — distinguishing their role from diagnostic assessment.

### Module 4 — Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Rehabilitation (7 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Explain the full biochemical and physiological rationale for sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise — covering autonomic restoration, cerebral autoregulation and CO₂ reactivity normalisation, exercise-induced BDNF upregulation and neuroplasticity, neuroinflammation reduction, and systemic deconditioning reversal.
2. Describe the heart-rate threshold (HRt) as the physiological marker of the autonomic/cerebrovascular ceiling, and explain why training at 80–90% of HRt (rather than at or above it) produces adaptive recovery.
3. Derive a complete FITT prescription from a BCTT HRt result, including worked calculations of the target HR band.
4. Apply the real-time symptom-monitoring rule (≥2-point rise = stop/reduce), distinguishing acceptable baseline noise from genuine threshold-crossing.
5. Progress the program safely using the re-test-driven progression algorithm — stepping the HR ceiling as tolerance recovers, and distinguishing the "duration first, then intensity" sequencing rule.
6. Select appropriate modalities for the deconditioned, dizzy, anxious or otherwise complex patient, and adapt the prescription to the presentation.
7. Recognise red flags that require regression, pause or urgent escalation, and communicate these to the referrer within scope.

### Module 5 — Phenotype-Specific Exercise Rehabilitation (7 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Describe the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each of the seven concussion phenotypes (vestibular, oculomotor, cervicogenic, autonomic/physiologic, cognitive/fatigue, affective, headache/migraine) and connect each mechanism to its clinical presentation.
2. Prescribe, dose and progress the exercise rehabilitation for each phenotype — including VOR×1/VOR×2 gaze stabilisation, habituation and dynamic balance for the vestibular phenotype; convergence, saccade, pursuit and accommodative facility drills for the oculomotor phenotype; cranio-cervical flexion, joint-position-error retraining and postural endurance work for the cervicogenic phenotype; and sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise (SSTAE) for the autonomic phenotype.
3. Explain the cerebellar neuroplasticity mechanism by which VOR gaze-stabilisation exercises restore vestibular function, and distinguish central vestibular adaptation from peripheral otoconia repositioning.
4. Describe the cervico-ocular reflex and explain how cervical proprioceptive injury contributes to dizziness and gaze instability after concussion.
5. Apply symptom-titrated dosing and a three-phase progression framework across all phenotypes, including the criteria for advancing, regressing and referring.
6. Identify the dominant phenotype driver when multiple phenotypes co-present, sequence the treatment plan accordingly, and layer secondary phenotype exercises appropriately.
7. Recognise the presentations — BPPV, manifest tropia, clinical depression, vertebrobasilar red flags — that require referral before or instead of exercise, and identify the appropriate specialist for each.

### Module 6 — Graded Return to Activity, Sport & Performance (5 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Apply the international graded return-to-sport (GRTS) strategy as a staged, symptom-monitored progression — with particular depth on stages 3 and 4 and what distinguishes each from the preceding stage.
2. Comply with the applicable concussion-guideline stand-down requirements and correctly apply both minimums (a calendar stand-down and a symptom-free period) to a real case, identifying which governs.
3. Explain the physiology of reconditioning — why aerobic base must precede sport-specific load, and the specific mechanisms by which running, multidirectional movement and contact-preparation each introduce qualitatively new physiological demands.
4. Design a concussion-specific return-to-running progression and team-sport conditioning program, applying objective progress, regress and readiness criteria governed by the 24-hour symptom rule.
5. Coordinate physical and cognitive load demands (return-to-learn and return-to-work pacing) and communicate an athlete's reconditioning readiness to the medical practitioner responsible for contact clearance.

### Module 7 — Persistent Symptoms & the Complex Case (7 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Define persistent post-concussion symptoms (PPCS) using the current consensus timeframe and terminology, and explain why PPCS is a heterogeneous clinical picture rather than a single diagnosis.
2. Describe the five pathophysiological mechanisms that drive persistence beyond the acute phase: sustained autonomic and cerebrovascular dysregulation, unresolved neuroinflammation, central sensitisation, the deconditioning spiral, and psychological maintaining factors including fear-avoidance and catastrophising.
3. Identify the clinical and demographic risk factors that predict prolonged recovery, and explain why early activity avoidance is itself a modifiable, iatrogenic risk factor.
4. Justify, from the evidence base, why graded sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise remains indicated — and actively therapeutic — in PPCS when a genuine symptom-limited threshold is confirmed.
5. Apply a four-step structured decision framework when a rehabilitation program stalls: reassess the BCTT threshold, screen for non-aerobic drivers, re-refer with data, and escalate red flags immediately.
6. Adapt rehabilitation for the comorbid, deconditioned and anxious patient using pacing, graded exposure and behaviour-change strategies within scope.
7. Recognise presentations that require referral beyond the exercise-physiology lane — including progressive presentations suggesting neurodegenerative concern — and describe what an accurate, in-scope response looks like in practice.

### Module 8 — Documentation, Communication & Referral (5 objectives)
By the end of this module, the learner will be able to:
1. Produce the four core scope-appropriate concussion-rehabilitation documents: assessment/BCTT record, session and progression tracking sheet, practitioner-to-referrer communication letter, and return-to-activity progress note — each as a real, structured artifact with scope-appropriate language.
2. Apply the record-within-scope principle: frame findings as exercise-physiology observations and recommendations, never as a diagnosis or medical clearance for return to contact.
3. Explain the three jobs every clinical record performs simultaneously — clinical continuity, inter-professional communication, and medico-legal accountability — and recognise why all three apply to exercise-physiology concussion records.
4. Apply the governing privacy principles to the health information collected, stored and disclosed in the course of concussion rehabilitation, including the specific obligations triggered by AI scribe tools.
5. Use AI-assisted documentation tools as an efficiency aid while retaining full personal professional responsibility for the accuracy and scope-appropriateness of every clinical note filed.

## A3 — Mission-Alignment Statement

ACSM's mission is to advance and integrate scientific research to provide educational and practical applications of exercise science and sports medicine. Concussion Rehab Mastery applies that mission to a specific opportunity: **a patient population that the 2022 international consensus has just placed inside the certified exercise physiologist's existing competency, and which ACSM-certified professionals are not currently serving.**

**The opportunity, stated plainly.** The 6th International Consensus Statement on Concussion in Sport (Patricios et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2023;57(11):695–711) replaced prolonged rest with early, individually prescribed sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise as first-line treatment. The consensus does not recommend generic activity. It recommends an intensity prescribed from a heart-rate threshold identified during a standardised, symptom-limited graded exercise test, monitored in real time and progressed against a re-test. Prescribed individualised exercise on that model shortened median recovery from 17 days to 13 days in the pivotal randomised trial (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics* 2019; n=103 adolescent athletes aged 13-18, P=.009), and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms.

Administering a graded exercise test, deriving a target heart-rate band from it, prescribing frequency, intensity, time and type against that band, and progressing the prescription on objective re-test data is the definitional core competency of a certified exercise physiologist. The consensus-recommended first-line treatment for concussion is, in operational terms, an exercise-physiology intervention. **The ACSM-EP and ACSM-CEP already hold the competency the treatment requires.** What this course supplies is the condition-specific application of it — which converts an existing credential into access to a new referral population.

**This is a service-line expansion, not a remediation.** The framing matters and is meant precisely. ACSM-certified professionals are not undertrained in concussion in any sense that implies risk or deficiency. Across the allied health professions, concussion knowledge is generally strong: a survey of 1,272 physiotherapists found 70% already held concussion training and averaged 13 out of 15 on concussion knowledge, with the gaps sitting specifically in return-to-play confidence and in knowing *when graded exertional training is indicated*. That is the signature of a workforce well trained on the pre-2022 model. The indication changed; the competency did not. Concussion Rehab Mastery is the update to the indication, delivered to professionals who already hold the skill it activates.

**Why the category is open.** ACSM's continuing-education catalogue and approved-provider list contain no concussion provision, while ACSM-EP and ACSM-CEP holders carry a mandatory 60-CEC three-year recertification cycle to fill. ACSM's own Hot Topic article, *Exercise & Rest in Concussion Recovery* (Hildenbrand and Herring, FACSM, 23 April 2025), states the position from inside the College: it identifies FITT-based exercise prescription for concussion as essential future work and directs clinicians to stay current with the evolving evidence. That is a published statement, by ACSM, that this precise translation task is unfinished. Concussion Rehab Mastery is offered as a contribution to that work, built for exercise professionals and structured around the FITT framework ACSM's own article names: frequency and time set by protocol, intensity set by the measured heart-rate threshold, type selected against patient presentation, and the whole prescription progressed against a standardised re-test. (Citing ACSM's public position is not a claim of ACSM endorsement of this course; no such endorsement is claimed or implied.)

Alignment is therefore direct on all three arms of the mission. **Scientific research is advanced and integrated:** the course is built on a 136-item peer-reviewed evidence base, 106 references carrying resolvable DOIs, anchored on the Buffalo protocol literature and the 2023 consensus. **Educational application:** 51 measurable objectives across 480 minutes of instruction, assessed by 87 questions distributed across the modules. **Practical application:** the learner finishes able to screen for contraindications, administer the BCTT or BCBT, record a heart-rate threshold, convert it to a FITT prescription, progress it on re-test data, and document the whole episode in a form the referring physician can act on — that is, able to open and run a concussion service line inside their existing credential. The course also teaches the scope boundary explicitly and repeatedly — diagnosis and medical clearance are not exercise-professional acts — which serves ACSM's interest in certified professionals practising safely inside their credential as that credential's application widens.

## A4 — Non-Competition Statement

**Concussion Rehab Mastery does not compete with any ACSM certification, specialty certification, workshop, or educational product. It is complementary by construction.**

The specific basis for that statement:

1. **No ACSM certification covers concussion.** ACSM-CPT, ACSM-EP, ACSM-CEP, ACSM-GEI and the ACSM specialty certifications contain no concussion assessment or concussion rehabilitation competency. Concussion is not a clinical population addressed in the certification job task analyses, and there is no ACSM-branded product a learner would forgo by taking this course.

2. **The course teaches nothing a certification teaches.** CRM contains no general exercise-testing curriculum, no ECG interpretation, no exercise-prescription fundamentals for cardiovascular, metabolic or pulmonary populations, no behaviour-change core, no anatomy or physiology foundation. It assumes those competencies as prerequisites and applies them to a population the certifications do not cover. The course is downstream of ACSM certification, not parallel to it.

3. **The course increases the value of an ACSM credential rather than substituting for one.** Its central argument to the learner is that the graded-exercise-test and prescription skills they already hold as an ACSM-certified professional are the skills the concussion consensus now requires. It presents ACSM certification as the prerequisite platform and this course as a population-specific extension of it. A learner cannot substitute CRM for an ACSM credential; CRM presumes one.

4. **The course does not credential.** CRM issues a certificate of completion. It does not confer a title, a protected designation, a registry listing, or a competency credential of any kind, and does not present itself as an alternative pathway to any ACSM certification or specialty certification.

5. **The market position is empty, not contested.** ACSM's continuing-education catalogue contains no concussion course, and no concussion provider appears on the approved-provider list. There is therefore no ACSM product, and no existing ACSM-approved provider offering, that CRM takes revenue or enrolment from. This is a category ACSM's own published commentary has identified as unfinished work, and CRM is offered as a contribution to filling it, not as a competing offer against anything ACSM sells.

## A7 — Assessment Method and Pass Mark

**Format.** Assessment is distributed, not terminal. There is no single final examination. **Eighty-seven (87) assessment questions** are embedded across the eight modules and interleaved with the instructional content, so that each block of teaching is immediately followed by the questions that test it.

**Distribution.**

| Module | Questions |
|---|---|
| 1 — Concussion for the Exercise Physiologist | 12 |
| 2 — Recognition, Red Flags & Scope of Practice | 11 |
| 3 — Assessment That Is the Treatment | 12 |
| 4 — Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Rehabilitation | 12 |
| 5 — Phenotype-Specific Exercise Rehabilitation | 10 |
| 6 — Graded Return to Activity, Sport & Performance | 10 |
| 7 — Persistent Symptoms & the Complex Case | 10 |
| 8 — Documentation, Communication & Referral | 10 |
| **Total** | **87** |

**Item design.** Items are multiple-choice with a single best answer. They are written against the module's stated learning objectives, and a substantial proportion are clinical-application items — the learner is given a patient scenario, a test result, a symptom response or a documentation extract and must select the correct clinical action. Every item, correct or incorrect, returns explanatory feedback that states why the selected option is right or wrong and refers the learner back to the underlying physiology or protocol. Recall-only items are used sparingly and only where a specific protocol parameter or red-flag threshold must be known verbatim.

**Pass mark.** **80%.** The threshold is applied at the module level. Enrolled learners may work through the modules in any order — access is governed by enrolment, not by a preceding score — and a learner may re-attempt a module assessment without limit. The 80% threshold is enforced at **certification**: it is checked against every module before a certificate is issued, so no certificate exists for a learner who has not met it on all eight.

**Completion requirement.** A certificate of completion is issued only when all eight modules have been completed and the 80% threshold has been met throughout. There is no partial-completion certificate.

**Rationale for 80% rather than a lower threshold.** The course teaches procedures with safety consequences — pre-test contraindication screening, symptom-limited test termination, red-flag escalation, and the boundary at which an exercise professional must stop and refer rather than proceed. A 70% threshold permits a learner to fail nearly a third of items and still be certified, which is not defensible for content of this type. The 80% gate at module level, rather than a single end-of-course average, prevents strong performance in one domain from masking a deficit in another.

## A9 — Target Credentials and Competency Fit

### Primary target credentials

**ACSM Certified Exercise Physiologist (ACSM-EP)** and **ACSM Certified Clinical Exercise Physiologist (ACSM-CEP)**.

### Why these two credentials specifically

The competency this course extends is not knowledge of concussion. It is the ability to administer a graded exercise test, identify a physiologically meaningful termination point, derive a prescription from that point, and progress it against re-test data. Those are the entry requirements, not the outcomes, of Concussion Rehab Mastery — and they are precisely what the ACSM-EP and ACSM-CEP credentials certify.

**ACSM-EP** requires a bachelor's degree in exercise science or an equivalent field, and certifies competency in conducting and interpreting physical fitness assessments, including submaximal and maximal graded exercise testing, and in developing individualised exercise prescriptions using the FITT framework, including heart-rate-based intensity prescription, for apparently healthy individuals and those with medically controlled conditions. Every procedural skill the course builds on sits inside that definition:

| CRM requirement | ACSM-EP certified competency |
|---|---|
| Administer a Balke-based symptom-limited graded treadmill test | Conducting graded exercise tests, including Balke-family protocols |
| Monitor HR and RPE continuously and apply termination criteria | Physiological monitoring during exercise testing; test termination indications |
| Derive a target HR band from a measured threshold | Heart-rate-based intensity prescription |
| Write a complete frequency/intensity/time/type prescription | FITT-based exercise prescription |
| Progress the prescription on objective re-test data | Exercise-program progression and reassessment |
| Screen pre-test contraindications and escalate red flags | Pre-participation health screening and risk stratification |

**ACSM-CEP** requires a bachelor's degree plus 1,200 hours of clinical exercise experience, or a master's degree plus 600 hours, and certifies the delivery of exercise testing and prescription to patients with clinical disease under a referral model. The CEP profile matches the concussion caseload closely on three counts. First, the concussion patient reaches the exercise professional by medical referral and returns to the referring physician for clearance — a workflow CEPs already operate inside and CPTs generally do not. Second, the physiological ceiling being tested is autonomic and cerebrovascular rather than cardiorespiratory, which requires a practitioner who is comfortable with a symptom-limited rather than a performance-limited endpoint. Third, persistent post-concussion symptoms are a chronic, deconditioned, frequently comorbid presentation with psychological maintaining factors — clinically the CEP's population profile.

For both credentials the practical value is a service-line extension rather than a topic upgrade — a new billable patient population reached through a competency the certificant already holds. The consensus now designates exercise testing as the gate to first-line concussion treatment. EPs and CEPs already hold that skill; this course makes it deliverable in a patient population that is currently being referred elsewhere or not treated at all. Nothing in the course asks the certificant to acquire a new core skill; it supplies the condition-specific protocol, indications and scope boundary that let an existing skill be applied to a population the certification does not address.

### Secondary target credentials

**ACSM Certified Personal Trainer (ACSM-CPT)** and **ACSM Certified Group Exercise Instructor (ACSM-GEI)**.

CPTs and GEIs are appropriate for a defined subset of the course. Modules 1, 2, 6 and 8 — mechanism, recognition and red flags, the graded return-to-activity strategy, and documentation and referral — are directly applicable to professionals who supervise activity in community, fitness and sport settings and who will encounter concussed clients regardless of whether they ever administer a BCTT. Module 2 in particular addresses a real safety function: recognising signs, escalating red flags, and knowing when to stop supervising and refer.

Modules 3, 4 and 5 assume graded-exercise-testing competency and clinical supervision arrangements that fall outside the CPT and GEI scope of practice. The course states this explicitly. CPT and GEI learners are directed to treat these modules as knowledge of the referral pathway — understanding what a concussion rehabilitation program consists of and who is qualified to deliver it — rather than as a licence to administer testing themselves. Scope boundaries are taught in Module 2 and reinforced in every subsequent module.

### Recertification fit

Both primary credentials carry a mandatory 60-CEC three-year recertification cycle. At the requested 8.0 CECs, Concussion Rehab Mastery covers roughly 13% of a cycle in a single course while adding a clinical population the certification itself does not address — new application of an existing competency, not repeated coverage of certification content. Because the ACSM CEC catalogue and approved-provider list currently contain no concussion provision, a certificant seeking this population has no ACSM-recognised route to it; the course is offered to open one.

## A10 — The Standardised Method Is Now Citable, and the Tool That Delivers It

**The gap this closes.** The 2022 consensus made a standardised, symptom-limited graded exercise test — and the individual heart-rate threshold it yields — the gate to first-line concussion treatment. But the College's own commentary (Hildenbrand & Herring, FACSM, 2025) records that the FITT translation of that method for concussion is unfinished, ACSM's CEC catalogue teaches no method for administering it, and the ACSM-EP / ACSM-CEP has, at present, no standardised, reproducible way to deliver that test-to-prescription pathway as a billable service line in a new patient population. The competency exists; the method and the tooling to operationalise it do not sit anywhere in ACSM provision.

**The method is now a published standard.** The pathway is fixed and citable: *A Standardised Clinical Protocol for Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Exercise Rehabilitation after Concussion (mild Traumatic Brain Injury)* (Lewis, 2026; open access, CC-BY-4.0; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21482634), anchored on the same evidence base ACSM's article points to (Patricios 2023; Leddy 2019 / 2021 / 2023). It specifies contraindication screening, BCTT/BCBT administration, HRt derivation, HRt-to-FITT conversion, re-test-governed progression, and the scope boundary. CRM is the course that teaches it; the protocol is the reviewable standard behind the course.

**The SST Trainer is the tool members use to deliver it.** Reading the protocol does not, by itself, put a service line in an EP's diary. The SST Trainer operationalises the protocol end to end: it runs the graded exercise test (BCTT/BCBT), records the **measured** individual heart-rate threshold, converts it into the sub-symptom-threshold aerobic prescription, monitors the between-visit home sessions, and returns a measured HR-threshold trajectory plus standardised progress and outcome reporting the referring physician can act on. The wedge is that the threshold is **measured, not estimated** — which is exactly what makes the EP's output defensible and billable in a population they are not currently seeing. Public demonstration: `/sst-trainer?clinic=DEMO00` and `/preseason/b/DEMO00`. Net effect for ACSM: a published method its members can cite, and a tool that turns an existing certified competency into a deliverable, measurable concussion service line — the precise translation task ACSM's own commentary flagged as outstanding.
