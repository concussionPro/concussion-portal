## 2. Canadian professional context — CES/CEP and the one genuine differentiator

Canada is not short of concussion education. Established Canadian concussion training is well regarded and predominantly **assessment-oriented** — recognition, sideline evaluation, screening, referral, and return-to-sport staging. CRM is not positioned against it and does not claim a gap in Canadian provision generally.

**The one defensible differentiator** is the layer beneath the staging: **heart-rate-threshold exercise prescription.** Administering a symptom-limited graded exercise test (BCTT/BCBT), deriving an individual heart-rate threshold, converting it into a complete FITT prescription, monitoring symptom response in real time, and progressing the physiological ceiling on re-test data is exercise-physiology work — it is what the 2022 Amsterdam consensus (Patricios et al., *BJSM*, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106898) actually gates treatment on, and it is not what assessment-oriented concussion training is built to teach. That work sits squarely inside CSEP-CEP scope and, in its conditioning and return-to-activity components, inside CSEP-CPT scope.

The supporting evidence is the international spine: early prescribed sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise shortened median recovery from 17 to 13 days (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics*, 2019, https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2018.4397; n=103, P=.009) and reduces persisting symptoms (Leddy et al., *Lancet Child Adolesc Health*, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1016/S2352-4642(21)00267-4; Leddy et al., *BJSM* meta-analysis, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106676). The wider point is not that practitioners are undertrained — concussion knowledge predates the 2022 shift; CRM is the update to the layer that moved.

That differentiating layer is now a published, citable standard rather than a course claim: *A Standardised Clinical Protocol for Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Exercise Rehabilitation after Concussion (mild Traumatic Brain Injury)* (Lewis, Z., 2026; Zenodo; open access, CC-BY-4.0; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21482634), which specifies exactly the exercise-prescription layer assessment-oriented training omits. The SST Trainer is the tool a CSEP-CEP uses to deliver it — running the graded exercise test, capturing the **measured** heart-rate threshold, issuing and monitoring the sub-symptom-threshold prescription, and producing a measured HR-threshold trajectory with standardised reporting (demo: `/sst-trainer?clinic=DEMO00`). This strengthens the equivalency case but does not change the sequence: the protocol and tool sit inside the ESSA AEP scope CSEP already recognises, and **Route 2 is still held until the ESSA endorsement is granted** (§4).

## 5. Canadian return-to-play legislation and guidance

Module 6 teaches a jurisdiction-neutral competency: identify the mandated stand-down requirements that apply, determine which minimum governs, refuse to progress the athlete past the stage the governance permits, and hand the contact-clearance decision to the physician. Canadian learners exercise that competency against:

- **Parachute Canada's *Canadian Guideline on Concussion in Sport*** as the baseline instrument.
- **Provincial concussion legislation** — including **Ontario's Rowan's Law (Concussion Safety), 2018** and its mandatory removal-from-sport and return-to-sport protocol requirements, and the equivalent requirements in other provinces.

## 6. Scope mapping, records, privacy and terms

- **Scope.** Diagnosis of concussion and return-to-contact clearance are medical acts in Canada as in Australia. Neither a CSEP-CEP nor a CSEP-CPT diagnoses concussion or clears an athlete for contact; the course states that in every module. Module 2 covers referral-in and red-flag triage; Module 8 covers recording findings as exercise-physiology observations and handing the clearance decision to the responsible physician.
- **Records and privacy.** Module 8 teaches the substantive principles (lawful collection, use/disclosure limits, cross-border transfer, security of health information, and the obligations triggered by AI scribe tools). For Canadian learners the corresponding instruments are **PIPEDA** and the applicable provincial health-information statutes (including Ontario's **PHIPA** and Alberta's **HIA**).
- **Data residency.** CEA hosts in Australia (Sydney region — Vercel `syd1` + Neon ap-southeast-2). Canadian learner personal data is processed outside Canada; this cross-border transfer is handled under the terms published at `/terms`.
