## INTERNAL — NOT PART OF THE SUBMISSION

> **Do not paste anything in this section into an email or a form.** It is strategic context for CEA only. The submission text begins at "EMAIL — listing request".

**The SESNZ listing is not the New Zealand play. The ACC supplier channel is.**

New Zealand's real opportunity is organisational: ACC-contracted concussion-service suppliers. Those suppliers carry mandated ACC884 six-monthly outcome reporting and are measured on client outcomes, quality and timeliness — which means training that measurably shortens recovery and produces defensible objective data is a procurement argument, not a CPD argument. That is an organisational deal (team licences, supplier-wide rollout), and it is where the revenue is.

**The structural strength to lean on in that channel:** ACC's mandated concussion team composition is medical, neuropsychology, psychology, occupational therapy and physiotherapy. None of those disciplines is trained in exercise testing. Yet the supplier is contractually obliged to deliver the guideline-concordant treatment — which since Amsterdam 2022 is exercise prescribed from an individually derived heart-rate threshold. The obligation exists; the competency to discharge it does not sit inside the mandated team as currently constituted. That is the gap the supplier has to solve, and it is solvable with training rather than a new hire.

**What the SESNZ listing is:** a cheap credibility adjunct to that channel. A public, member-facing listing on a national professional body's CPD page makes the course easier to justify inside a supplier's procurement conversation. It is low cost, low effort, plausibly free, and worth doing — but it is an adjunct, not the main play. Keep the effort proportionate: send the short email below, do not build a submission pack for it.

## POSITIONING SPINE — the argument this listing carries

One idea governs every line of the email below, and it must not be misstated.

Amsterdam 2022 moved concussion from a **rest** condition to an **exercise-prescription** condition. Treatment is now gated on exercise testing and on an individually derived heart-rate threshold. Prescribed individualised exercise shortened median recovery from 17 days to 13 days in the pivotal randomised trial (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatrics* 2019; n=103 adolescent athletes aged 13-18, P=.009), and reduces the incidence of persisting symptoms.

Every relevant profession completed its concussion training **before** that shift. The evidence for that is not anecdotal: a survey of **1,272 physiotherapists** found that **70% already held concussion training** and scored an average of **13 out of 15** on concussion knowledge — with the specific gaps sitting in return-to-play confidence and in knowing **when graded exertional training is indicated**. High knowledge, current-to-the-old-model. That is the shape of the problem.

**The line to land: they are not ignorant — they are current on the old model.** CRM is the update to the new model.

**Never** frame this as remedial education, as a knowledge deficit, or as fixing ignorance. To a national professional body that framing insults its members and gets the listing declined. The correct register is: the evidence moved, here is the piece that moved, here is where practitioners can pick it up.

# ONE-PAGE SUMMARY (paste into the email or attach)

## Concussion Rehab Mastery — Concussion Education Australia

**What it is.** An online, self-paced course training exercise professionals to deliver the exercise-based treatment of concussion that international consensus now places first-line: sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise, prescribed from a heart-rate threshold derived by graded exercise testing.

**Delivery mode.** Wholly online, asynchronous, self-paced, on CEA's own platform. Individual logins, per-module progress tracking, no live attendance. Progress persists between sessions; access continues after completion so the material works as a practice reference.

**Duration and CPD points.**

| # | Module | Duration | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concussion for the Exercise Physiologist — *Functional Injury, the Neurometabolic Cascade & Why Exercise Is Now First-Line* | 60 min | 1.0 |
| 2 | Recognition, Red Flags & Scope of Practice | 30 min | 0.5 |
| 3 | Assessment That Is the Treatment — *The Buffalo Concussion Treadmill/Bike Test* | 90 min | 1.5 |
| 4 | Sub-Symptom-Threshold Aerobic Rehabilitation — *The Biochemistry of Recovery, the FITT Prescription, and Progressing the Physiological Ceiling* | 90 min | 1.5 |

Points are claimed at 1 point per hour of assessed instructional content — the same basis the SESNZ scheme uses. Against a 20-point annual requirement, one completion covers 8.

**Assessment.** 87 questions, delivered **within each module** rather than as a final exam, so a learner cannot advance past a protocol step they haven't understood. **80% pass mark** at module level. Questions test protocol accuracy (the per-minute BCTT increments, the two termination criteria), physiological reasoning, clinical decision-making, and scope discipline. Every item returns a written rationale. Certification requires all eight modules at or above the pass mark.

**Target audience (NZ).** CEPNZ-registered Clinical Exercise Physiologists; SESNZ members working in physiology support and sport science roles; exercise scientists and kinesiologists in clinical, ACC-funded and return-to-work rehabilitation; strength and conditioning practitioners carrying return-to-training reconditioning responsibility in provincial, academy and professional programmes; physiotherapists with a sport and exercise medicine caseload who want the graded exertion testing and aerobic prescription component at physiological depth. Assumes undergraduate exercise physiology — cardiorespiratory response to exercise, heart-rate and RPE monitoring, graded exercise test principles, FITT programming. The course applies those to concussion; it does not teach them.

**Learning outcomes.** 51 assessed learning objectives across the eight modules. A representative selection:

- Justify why sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise is now first-line care rather than prolonged rest, citing the evidence base that drove the paradigm shift.
- List the red flags that mandate urgent emergency escalation, and describe the pathophysiological mechanism — intracranial haemorrhage, second-impact syndrome and cervical spine injury — that makes each a medical emergency.
- Administer the standardised Balke-based Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test, including the precise per-minute incline and speed increments, continuous heart rate/RPE/symptom monitoring, and the two distinct test-termination criteria.
- Record the heart rate at symptom threshold (HRt), describe what it represents physiologically — the point where impaired cerebral autoregulation and blunted cerebrovascular CO₂ reactivity can no longer meet exertional demand — and distinguish it from the voluntary-exhaustion endpoint.
- Convert the HRt into an individualised sub-symptom-threshold aerobic prescription using the 80–90% rule, including frequency, duration and mode.
- Apply the real-time symptom-monitoring rule (≥2-point rise = stop/reduce), distinguishing acceptable baseline noise from genuine threshold-crossing.
- Describe the neurophysiological mechanism underlying each of the seven concussion phenotypes (vestibular, oculomotor, cervicogenic, autonomic/physiologic, cognitive/fatigue, affective, headache/migraine) and connect each to its clinical presentation.
- Apply the international graded return-to-sport strategy as a staged, symptom-monitored progression, with particular depth on stages 3 and 4.
- Apply a four-step structured decision framework when a rehabilitation programme stalls: reassess the BCTT threshold, screen for non-aerobic drivers, re-refer with data, and escalate red flags immediately.
- Apply the record-within-scope principle: frame findings as exercise-physiology observations and recommendations, never as a diagnosis or medical clearance for return to contact.
