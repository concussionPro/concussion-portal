# ACC as the Funder — Pitch Pack

**Audience:** ACC service design, clinical leadership, and System Commissioning & Performance. **Not** a contracted supplier.
**Prepared:** 20 July 2026
**Source documents:** `ACC-AS-BUYER.md` and `NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md`. Every factual claim below is carried from those two documents, which hold the primary URLs. Nothing new is asserted here without a pointer back to one of them.

## READ THIS FIRST — how this pack differs from the supplier pack

`NZ-ACC-SUPPLIER-PACK.md` and the public `/acc` page sell to one of the thirteen contracted Concussion Services suppliers. That pitch turns on the supplier's own problems: unpaid ACC884 reporting load, a Cl. 5.2.1 team composition that was not trained for exercise-threshold testing, and the six-monthly Māori-disaggregated report that is almost certainly hand-assembled. Those are real and they sell.

**None of them is a funder's problem.** ACC does not care how long an ACC884 takes a supplier to write; it pays a fixed hourly rate and the admin burden is priced into it. Reusing that framing here would read as a vendor complaining on a customer's behalf.

The funder-facing problem is different, larger, and entirely ACC's own. It is set out below. If any sentence in this pack could equally have appeared in the supplier pack, it is in the wrong document.

# PART 1 — THE PROBLEM STATEMENT

*One page. Written for an ACC service-design or clinical audience.*

## ACC funds a treatment it has endorsed but never defined

ACC's *Sport Concussion in New Zealand: National Guidelines*, updated January 2026 and built on the 2022 Amsterdam consensus, states that **"strong evidence supports the benefits of aerobic exercise at a level that does not worsen symptoms during the activity as an early intervention treatment within a recovery plan."** That is an endorsement, and it is current — the guideline is six months old and its expert panel includes ACC's own Injury Prevention Partner for Sport and TBI (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §4).

ACC also funds that treatment. Two line items in the Concussion Services Service Schedule pay for it directly, both at **$155.27 per hour**:

- **TBI22**, cl. 5.8.2.1.2 — *"Assessment of exercise tolerance and/or functional capacity for undertaking programmes."*
- **TBI26**, Stage 2 — *"...return to work, return to School, exercise programmes, vestibular therapy..."*

**What is missing is the method.** Across the full Service Schedule (2,406 lines) and the Operational Guidelines (789 lines), the strings *heart rate*, *threshold*, *Buffalo*, *treadmill*, *sub-symptom*, *exertion*, *bpm*, *aerobic*, *SCAT*, *Amsterdam* and *CISG* appear **zero times**. The January 2026 guideline's own return-to-activity ladder is qualitative throughout — *"gentle exercise (i.e. walking around the house)"*, *"increase intensity of exercise guided by symptoms"* — with the same eleven strings absent (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §4).

So the position is: **endorsed, funded, and method-unspecified.**

And the contract makes that explicit rather than accidental. Cl. 5.4.5 requires that outcomes be measured using a validated objective clinical measure; cl. 5.4.6 states that the tools used *"will be standardised measurements selected by the Supplier."* Cl. 5.5.4.1 requires *"an appropriate screening tool"* and names none. No instrument is named anywhere in either document (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §1).

**Thirteen suppliers therefore each decide, independently and without visibility of one another, what "a level that does not worsen symptoms" means in practice.** One may derive an individually measured heart-rate threshold from a symptom-limited graded exercise test, as the Amsterdam statement describes for clinicians with access to exercise testing. Another may apply an age-estimated 85% of maximum heart rate, as bpac's GP-facing guidance uses — but as a *discharge criterion*, not a dosing method. A third may work entirely from symptom report and the "+3 rule", as ACC's own patient education sheet ACC8319 does. All three are defensible readings of the same guideline sentence. All three are billed under the same code at the same rate.

## Why this becomes a measurement problem, not just a variation problem

ACC benchmarks these same thirteen suppliers against **"National Average or one deviation higher/lower"** on six KPIs computed from its own claims data — service progression, timeliness of entry, and three return-to-work measures (Service Schedule cl. 12 and Part B Table 4; Operational Guidelines Table 4). Suppliers cannot influence the measurement; it is ACC-side data. Cl. 12.3.1 triggers scrutiny where a supplier *"differs materially from that of comparable suppliers"*, and cl. 13.4 permits ACC to share **non-anonymised** supplier performance with all other contracted suppliers (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §3).

That comparison assumes the suppliers are doing comparable things. On the exercise component, there is nothing in the contract that makes them comparable. **To the extent that method varies arbitrarily between suppliers, the benchmark is partly measuring method variation rather than performance variation** — and a supplier flagged under cl. 12.3.1 may be flagged for a protocol choice ACC never specified.

## Why this is ACC's to own

A supplier cannot fix this. Cl. 5.4.6 gives each supplier the right to choose, and each has met its obligation the moment it chooses something. There is no mechanism inside the contract by which thirteen independent choices converge, and no supplier has any incentive to adopt another's method.

**Unwarranted variation in a treatment the funder has endorsed, priced, and benchmarked is a funder-level problem by construction.** It also happens to be the objective ACC set itself publicly in December 2019, when it ended the Axis Sports Concussion Centre of Excellence pilot and stated it was looking at delivering *"a nationally scalable clinical pathway and concussion assessment tool"* (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §2). Six and a half years on, that sentence is still open on the exercise component.

# PART 2 — THE PROPOSAL

## A measured comparison ACC can own

The proposal is not a product sale and not a validation study. It is **a comparison, designed to be answerable from data ACC already holds, that tells ACC whether standardising the exercise-tolerance method changes anything it cares about.**

If the answer is no, ACC has bought a cheap negative on a question it has left open since 2019, and can stop asking it. That outcome is acceptable and should be stated as acceptable up front.

### 2.1 Design in one paragraph

Take a small number of contracted Concussion Services suppliers. Standardise the method used under TBI22 and TBI26 in some of them, leave the remainder to continue under cl. 5.4.6 as they do today, and read out the difference on ACC's existing claim-duration and return-to-work measures over a fixed window with a pre-declared decision date.

This suits ACC's demonstrated preference. Its August 2025 primary-care procurement sought **"at least three entities"** to implement and manage interventions — ACC deliberately runs parallel interventions and compares them rather than backing one (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §4). This proposal is that pattern applied to a treatment ACC already funds.

### 2.2 Which suppliers

**Four to six of the thirteen**, selected by ACC, not by us. The selection criteria that matter:

- **Volume.** Enough Stage 1 and Stage 2 concussion claims per site to read a duration difference within twelve months.
- **Multi-site where possible.** Habit Health, TBI Health Group, New Zealand Health Group and Axis Sports Medicine Specialists are the multi-site names on the awarded list (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §1); multi-site suppliers give ACC within-organisation comparison as well as between-organisation.
- **A pre-declared split.** Half standardised, half continuing as-is. Which half is standardised should be decided before any baseline data is examined and recorded in writing.

Nothing about the design requires ACC to disclose supplier identities publicly, and nothing requires a new contract variation — the standardised arm is adopting a specific method under a clause that already permits any method.

### 2.3 What is standardised

Exactly one thing: **how the exercise level under TBI22 and TBI26 is determined and recorded.**

In the standardised arm, participating clinicians:

1. Derive the exercise prescription from an **individually measured heart-rate threshold** obtained by symptom-limited graded exertion testing, rather than from an age-estimated maximum or from symptom report alone — the method the Amsterdam consensus describes for clinicians with access to exercise testing (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §4).
2. Prescribe against that measured threshold, and re-derive it on repeat testing rather than progressing by duration.
3. Record the threshold, the prescribed dose, the sessions actually completed and the heart rate actually achieved, in a consistent structured format, so that "what was delivered" is a fact rather than a recollection.
4. Complete the same training before starting, so that the method means the same thing in each site.

**Everything else stays exactly as it is.** No change to referral routes, staging, funding codes, the Maximum Funding Limit, ACC884 content, or the six-monthly Supplier Report. The comparison arm changes nothing at all.

The clinician cohort is **physiotherapists and occupational therapists**. Exercise physiologists are not an option: "exercise physiologist" and "exercise physiology" appear zero times across the Allied Health, Clinical Services, Vocational Rehabilitation, Pain Management, Training for Independence, Community Rehabilitation, TBI Residential and Concussion schedules, and there is no ACC vocational classification for them (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §4). Any proposal that assumes a CEP workforce is dead on arrival.

### 2.4 What ACC measures — existing data only

**No new instruments. No new PROMs. No new reporting obligation on suppliers.** Every endpoint below is already computed by ACC from its own claims data under Part B Table 4:

| Endpoint | Source | Why it is the right one |
|---|---|---|
| Average weeks of weekly compensation after Stage 1 entry | KPI 5, RTW efficiency | The closest single number to "claim duration" ACC already publishes internally |
| Proportion receiving weekly compensation >26 weeks after Stage 1 entry | KPI 4, RTW effectiveness | The long-tail measure; where cost concentrates |
| Proportion sustaining return to work at least three months | KPI 6, RTW sustainability | Guards against a duration gain that does not hold |
| Proportion entering Stage 2 from Stage 1 | KPI 1, service progression | Detects whether standardisation shifts case mix rather than outcomes |
| Total cost per claim against the $4,120 excl GST Maximum Funding Limit | ACC claims data | The funder's own currency, directly |

Two supporting reads, also from data ACC holds: the corporate RTW measures at 28 days and 10 weeks (Service Agreement Table 1), and median time from service commencement to ACC884 submission, which suppliers already report six-monthly.

**The single design commitment that matters: the endpoint list is fixed and written down before the first client is seen.** BIST's failure was not that it measured the wrong things; it was that it never measured these things at all.

### 2.5 Period and decision point

- **Months 0–2 — design and baseline.** Endpoints fixed in writing. Arms allocated. Baseline pulled from ACC claims data for the same suppliers over the preceding 24 months. Training delivered to the standardised arm.
- **Months 3–14 — delivery.** Twelve months of recruitment. No protocol changes, no extension for "further testing of changes based on user feedback" — that clause is precisely what turned a one-year BIST pilot into a four-year one (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §6.5).
- **Months 15–20 — follow-through.** The 26-week and three-month-sustained measures require the tail. This is why the decision date is at month 20 and not month 14, and it should be stated in the first meeting so nobody is surprised by it.
- **Month 20 — decision date, declared at the outset.**

**The decision rule, agreed in advance:**

- **If the standardised arm shows a materially shorter average weeks of weekly compensation with sustained RTW held or improved** — ACC has an evidenced case to specify the method, whether by naming it in the next Concussion Services re-tender (the contract term runs to 30 June 2027, extendable one year, so the drafting window is real), by attaching it to a funded code, or by making the training a condition of participation as it did in the primary-care pathway.
- **If there is no material difference** — ACC publishes the null, closes the 2019 question, and stops paying attention to method. We accept that outcome and say so now.
- **If the standardised arm is worse** — the same publication obligation applies.

No third option in which the pilot is extended to test refinements.

### 2.6 What ACC is being asked to contribute

Deliberately little, because the proposal's credibility depends on not asking for much:

1. **Access to its own claims data** for the participating suppliers, for the pre-declared endpoints, at baseline and at readout. This is the substantive ask.
2. **A named owner inside ACC** who holds the decision date. Every prior concussion initiative lost its owner (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §6.4).
3. **A view on who pays the clinician** for the additional assessment time in the standardised arm. This has to be answered before anything starts — see §6.
4. **Nothing in kind from the tool vendor's side is being sold to ACC.** ACC is not being asked to buy software.

# PART 3 — WHY THIS IS NOT BIST

This section exists because ACC has been here before, with a better-credentialled team, and the pack should say so before ACC does.

## 3.1 What actually happened with BIST

The Brain Injury Screening Tool was developed by Prof Alice Theadom's group at the **TBI Network, Auckland University of Technology**. **AUT's TBI Network funded the development study; ACC did not.** The PLoS One funding statement reads *"The TBI Network at the Auckland University of Technology provided reimbursement to participants. No additional external funding was received for this study."* Anyone who tells ACC it funded BIST will be corrected in the room (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §1).

What ACC did contribute was a co-author from day one — Natalie Hardaker, ACC Injury Prevention Partner for Sport and TBI, who also sits on the National Concussion Guidelines expert panel — and, later, a funded pathway. BIST became the assessment instrument inside ACC's **Primary Care Concussion Pilot**, live since May 2022 across WellSouth, Pegasus and ProCare, with **869 assessments completed by March 2024** and an expansion that year to subcontracted physiotherapists.

The adoption template had four parts, and all four worked:

1. An ACC subject-matter expert named as a co-author from the first paper.
2. **Funded consultations** — *"clients are eligible for an initial GP appointment and two fully-funded follow-ups, with a BIST assessment completed at each appointment."* The clinician was never asked to absorb the time.
3. A **PHO as the contracting and payment vehicle** — *"PHOs will be responsible for paying physiotherapists for the BIST assessments."*
4. **Education mandatory as a condition of participation** — participating physiotherapists must *"complete the education requirements for the pathway."*

## 3.2 Where it stopped, and why

Four published papers: development and factor structure (PLoS One 2021), Rasch analysis (BMC Neurology 2021), a correction, and test-retest reliability (BMJ Open 2022). **Every one of them psychometric.** Validity, scaling, reliability.

**Zero outcome studies. Zero cost analyses. Zero claim-duration analyses. No published pilot evaluation at all** — nothing in PubMed, nothing on acc.co.nz — four years after first publication and four years into a live funded pilot with 869 assessments behind it.

And the consequence is visible in ACC's own documents. BIST is named nowhere in the Concussion Services contract, nowhere in the January 2026 National Concussion Guidelines, and nowhere in ACC's corporate strategy. Full-text extraction of Strategy 2026–2029, Statement of Intent 2026–2030, Service Agreement 2026/27 and the Turnaround Plan 2025/26 returns **zero occurrences** of concussion, brain injury, TBI or BIST across all four. ACC has published nothing about the pilot since 4 March 2024 — twenty-eight months (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §1, §6.1).

## 3.3 The lesson, stated plainly

**ACC will not scale something demonstrated only in psychometric terms.** Its entire published performance frame is claim-side: return to work at 28 days, 10 weeks, nine months and one year, and the long-term claims pool. A funder cannot scale what has not been shown to work in its own currency, and four years of demonstrating that an instrument measures consistently never produced a single number ACC reports to its Minister.

This is a research-design failure, not a sales failure. It is also entirely avoidable.

## 3.4 How this proposal is built so it cannot repeat it

| BIST failure mode | How this proposal is constructed differently |
|---|---|
| Evidence produced was psychometric | **The only endpoints are ACC's own claim-duration and RTW measures.** No psychometric endpoint is proposed, funded, or accepted as a substitute. There is no validity study in this design |
| No published evaluation after four years | **The readout is the deliverable.** A pre-declared endpoint list, a fixed decision date, and a publication obligation that applies equally to a null or negative result |
| Pilot extended indefinitely on user feedback | **Month 20 decision date, fixed at the outset**, with no extension mechanism for protocol refinement |
| No commercial vehicle whose survival depended on adoption | An operating company, not a grant-funded research team, carries delivery and maintenance — and says so as a fact about incentives, not a virtue |
| No owner inside ACC across an executive turnover | A named ACC owner is requested as a condition of starting, not as a courtesy |
| Tool solved a compliance problem no supplier had | This targets the exercise-method gap under TBI22/TBI26, where **ACC has to specify** — not the outcome-measure choice, which cl. 5.4.6 has already delegated to suppliers (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §6.6) |

**The one-sentence version for a meeting:** *BIST proved an instrument measures consistently and never proved it changes a claim. We are proposing to skip straight to the second question, and to accept the answer either way.*

# PART 4 — THE VEHICLE QUESTION

## 4.1 Why a vehicle is required at all

ACC's stated buying shape is aggregators. Its August 2025 primary-care procurement sought entities with **"coverage across multiple general practice clinics (i.e. meso-level organisations)"** and **"meaningful clinician influence, with potential to scale"** (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §4). Every ACC concussion initiative that reached funded delivery ran through an institution: PHOs for the primary-care pilot, Christchurch Hospital for Wayfind-TBI, AUT for BIST.

A single Australian software and CPD company is not that shape and should not pretend to be. **The vehicle question is therefore not a formality — it is the first thing ACC will ask, and having no answer is the fastest way to end the conversation.**

There is also no shortcut around it. ACC has no approved-product, approved-technology or endorsed-tool scheme — a confirmed negative from the primary contract documents. ACC111 vendor registration is payment plumbing; developer.acc.co.nz is technical API conformance and confers no clinical endorsement; Pae Hokohoko is a discovery catalogue with no committed spend (`NZ-ACC-PROCUREMENT-REALITY.md` §1, `ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §4). There is nothing to get listed on that would substitute for a partner.

## 4.2 The four options

### Option A — a PHO (WellSouth, Pegasus or ProCare)

**For.** The proven template. All three already hold ACC concussion pathway agreements, already subcontract physiotherapists, already handle payment, and already enforce mandatory education as a condition of participation. Live published clinical contacts exist (`concussion@wellsouth.org.nz`, `concussion@pegasus.org.nz`, `concussion@procare.co.nz`). Nothing would need inventing.

**Against.** PHOs sit in **primary care**. TBI22 and TBI26 are Concussion Services contract codes delivered by contracted rehabilitation suppliers, not by GPs. Routing an exercise-threshold comparison through a PHO means either changing the setting (and losing the claim-linked endpoints that make this work) or asking a PHO to coordinate suppliers it has no relationship with. There is also unresolved uncertainty about whether the Primary Care Concussion Pilot is even still running.

**Verdict.** Right template, wrong setting for this specific question.

### Option B — a cohort of contracted Concussion Services suppliers

**For.** These are the entities that actually deliver TBI22 and TBI26 and generate the claims that produce the endpoints. Several are multi-site with genuine clinician influence — the meso-level shape ACC asked for. Cl. 5.4.6 already permits each of them to adopt a specified method without any contract variation. This is the only option where the intervention lands exactly where the funding gap is.

**Against.** A cohort is not a legal entity. Someone must convene it, hold the data-sharing arrangement, and be accountable for the readout — and thirteen suppliers ranked against each other on non-anonymised performance data under cl. 13.4 have limited natural appetite for collaborating. Convening is plausibly ACC's own job, which turns the proposal into something ACC must want rather than something it can simply approve.

**Verdict.** Necessary as the delivery surface. Insufficient as the vehicle.

### Option C — an academic partner (AUT TBI Network)

**For.** The TBI Network is the single most credible NZ concussion research institution, holds the BIST relationship, and has an established working history with ACC including a co-authored instrument. It satisfies the Innovation Fund eligibility criterion requiring *"existing support from clinicians, clients/client groups, a New Zealand-based institution or organisation"* — which every one of the eight recipients across both rounds met as an NZ entity (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §3). It supplies the independence a vendor cannot supply for its own readout. And it is the natural custodian of a published null result, which this design must be willing to produce.

**Against.** The obvious risk is reproducing BIST's failure mode: an academic partner's instinct is a validation study, and a validation study is precisely what this proposal must refuse. That has to be addressed in the first conversation, not the fifth. There is also a candour requirement — the same group whose BIST work stalled would be asked to run a design explicitly built around why it stalled.

**Verdict.** The strongest single anchor, conditional on the endpoint discipline being agreed in writing before anything else.

### Option D — a professional body (Physiotherapy New Zealand)

**For.** Genuine clinician reach across the exact workforce, an existing ACC relationship (it partnered on concussion PROM trials per the December 2022 ICIP Cabinet paper), and a natural home for the training component.

**Against.** The PROM trial outcome is entirely unknown — it appears in no published source and must not be cited as precedent. A professional body has reach but no claims data, no research infrastructure, and no delivery contract. It is a distribution partner, not a study sponsor.

**Verdict.** Valuable in phase two, for scaling training. Not the vehicle.

## 4.3 Recommendation

**AUT TBI Network as the named NZ anchor and study sponsor, with a cohort of contracted Concussion Services suppliers as the delivery surface, and ACC providing the claims data and a named owner.**

Three roles, cleanly separated:

- **AUT** holds the design, the endpoint discipline, the analysis and the publication. It is the NZ institutional partner that satisfies both the Innovation Fund criterion and ACC's aggregator preference.
- **The supplier cohort** delivers, in a setting where the funded codes and the claim endpoints already exist.
- **CEA** supplies the standardised method, the training that makes it consistent between sites, and the software that records what was actually delivered. **It does not hold the evaluation and should not.**

The precondition, agreed before anything is signed: **the endpoints are ACC's claim-duration and return-to-work measures, and no psychometric endpoint substitutes for them.** If that cannot be agreed, the proposal should not proceed, because it will become BIST.

# PART 6 — WHAT ACC WILL ASK THAT WE CANNOT ANSWER YET

Blunt list. Every item here should be volunteered before ACC finds it, because ACC has funded enough of these to recognise the pattern — and its own BIST co-author and one of its own contracted suppliers have been doing concussion tool development inside the system for five years (`ACC-AS-BUYER.md` §6).

**"What outcome data do you have?"**
**None.** No claim-duration, return-to-work, cost or symptom-trajectory data in any population, let alone a New Zealand one. The evidence supports the *protocol* — Leddy et al. 2019 randomised 103 adolescents aged 13–18 to early sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise and found median recovery of 13 days versus 17 (P = .009). **The software delivering the protocol has no outcome evidence and we will not claim otherwise.** The defensible claim is delivery fidelity and method consistency. Nothing about outcome. This is precisely why the proposal is a measured comparison and not a sale.

**"Who else uses it?"**
**Nobody.** No reference customer in New Zealand or Australia. Every ACC precedent had an institutional partner attached at proposal stage; we currently have none, which is why Part 4 is in this pack.

**"What is your New Zealand accreditation?"**
**There is none to hold.** ACC operates no approved-product or endorsed-tool scheme — a confirmed negative. That cuts both ways: nothing to point at, and no gate to clear.

**"Is it published?"**
**No.** An mTBI manuscript is prepared for submission. It has not been submitted, is not under review, and is not published. It must never be described as any of those things.

**"What are your professional endorsements?"**
Concussion Clinical Mastery holds an Osteopathy Australia endorsement. **The ESSA endorsement is pending, belongs to a different product, and is not claimed.** No exercise physiologist authored the courses. There is no New Zealand professional-body recognition for the training, and no confirmed pathway to one.

**"Is it a medical device?"**
**Open.** The Therapeutic Products Act 2023 was repealed; the Medicines Act 1981 governs, and s3A turns on stated intended purpose. A tool that outputs a heart-rate-threshold-derived dose sits closer to that line than a documentation aid does. No Medsafe software guidance exists. A written view from `devices@health.govt.nz` has not been obtained. An Australian vendor would also need a New Zealand sponsor for WAND notification.

**"Who pays the clinician for the extra assessment time?"**
**Unresolved, and it is the question that decides whether this is real.** BIST got uptake in primary care because ACC funded the consultations; it did not spread into the Concussion Services contract because that contract is strictly fee-for-service against fixed codes with no additional fee invoiceable for any report. TBI22 is billed at an hourly rate, so a longer assessment is in principle billable within the $4,120 per-client Maximum Funding Limit — but whether that holds in practice, and who absorbs the training time, has not been tested with any supplier or with ACC.

**"What does it cost ACC?"**
**No model exists.** No cost-per-claim analysis, no pricing for a multi-supplier deployment, no estimate of the training cost across a cohort. This has to be built before a second meeting.

**"Which suppliers have agreed to take part?"**
**None have been approached about this.** The supplier conversations that exist are commercial and framed around the supplier's own reporting burden — a different pitch to a different audience.

**"Is the Primary Care Concussion Pilot still running?"**
**We do not know**, and neither does anything published. Twenty-eight months of silence. This should be asked, not assumed, and the three PHO concussion addresses will answer it faster than an OIA.

**"Where is the data hosted?"**
Australia satisfies Service Schedule cl. 14.1.2, which permits New Zealand and/or Australia and bars everything else. Region pinning is a build item that must be confirmed complete, with a subprocessor list, before any privacy answer is given to anyone.

**"Are you a health agency under the Health Information Privacy Code?"**
**Probably, and it is unresolved.** HIPC 2020 cl. 4(2)(j) defines a health agency to include *"an agency which provides services in respect of health information"*. No case law or OPC guidance applying that to a SaaS vendor was found. A New Zealand privacy lawyer's opinion is the highest-value outstanding legal item.

## The one thing that must not be said in any of these conversations

**That nobody has tried this.** ACC's own Injury Prevention Partner co-authored BIST. One of its thirteen contracted suppliers co-authored it too. ACC has run a concussion pilot in 2017–2019, another from 2022, an Innovation Fund award into an ED concussion tool, and PROM trials with Physiotherapy New Zealand. They have tried, more than once, and they know exactly why none of it scaled.

**Naming those reasons first is the only credible opening.**
