## 9. Brand voice

### Tone (the dial)
- **Clinician-to-clinician.** Peer, not preacher. You're talking to other AHPRA registrants who already have clinical training.
- **Evidence-based without being academic.** Reference guidelines, papers, protocols — but in service of clinical action, not as proof of intellectual rigor.
- **Direct.** No fluff, no hype, no "transformative" / "revolutionary" / "game-changing".
- **Australian.** Australian English spelling. Australian context. Australian sport (AFL, NRL, A-League, surf, MMA). Australian regulatory framing (AHPRA, AIS, SMA, ACSEP, APA, RACGP).
- **Specific.** "Cut return-to-play decision time from 30 minutes to 5" beats "save time on return-to-play decisions". Numbers, scenarios, named protocols.
- **Honest about uncertainty.** Concussion has unknowns. We say so. We don't promise certainty we don't have.

### Voice characteristics (Zac specifically)
- First-person when appropriate ("I had a patient last week who…") — founder authority
- Comfortable with clinical jargon when audience is clinical (don't dumb it down for clinicians)
- Will name competitors and specifics when relevant
- Not afraid of long sentences when the structure earns it
- Hates marketing-speak

### Anti-examples (NEVER write like this)
- ❌ "Transform your practice with cutting-edge concussion training!"
- ❌ "Unlock the secrets of effective concussion management"
- ❌ "Game-changing protocols every clinician needs to know"
- ❌ "Revolutionary approach to concussion care"
- ❌ "Take your practice to the next level"
- ❌ Generic listicles ("5 things every clinician should know about concussion")
- ❌ Anything that could appear in a LinkedIn engagement-bait post

### Good examples (write like this)
- ✅ "Half of GPs in Australia don't feel confident managing concussion when symptoms persist past two weeks (UQ research, 2025). The protocol that bridges the gap is buffered exertion testing — here's how to run it in clinic in 15 minutes."
- ✅ "If you're working with junior football and someone goes down with a head knock, the AFL's 21-day community return-to-play minimum applies regardless of your symptom assessment. Here's what that means for your clearance letter."
- ✅ "The new ANZ Concussion Guidelines dropped in November 2025. Three things changed for community clinicians. Here's the actual changes, not the marketing version."

## 10. Channels & content formats

### LinkedIn (primary B2B channel)
- 1–3 posts/week
- Multi-image and native PDF post types perform best in healthcare (6.10–6.60% engagement)
- Best times: Tuesday–Thursday 6–7am or 6–8pm AEST
- Hooks: clinical scenario, regulatory update, contrarian take, personal practice story
- Always end with: implication for the reader, optional CTA (only sometimes — overusing CTAs kills reach)

### Email (highest conversion channel)
- Healthcare email open rate 41–44.6%, $36 ROI per $1
- Subject lines: under 50 chars, no exclamation marks, specific over clever
- Tone: founder-to-peer, conversational, ends with reply-friendly question or hard CTA depending on intent
- One email = one CTA. No exceptions.

### Blog / SEO (compounding asset channel)
- Format optimised for AI extraction: definition blocks, step-by-step blocks, comparison tables, FAQ blocks, statistic blocks
- Schema markup: FAQ, HowTo, Article (with author = Zac Lewis)
- Target queries: regulatory keywords (SCAT6, ANZ guidelines), comparison keywords (SCAT5 vs SCAT6), how-to keywords (how to score VOMS), clinician-specific (concussion CPD osteopath)
- Publish to `/blog`, also to `/llms.txt` for AI agent discoverability

### X / Twitter (low priority)
- Optional thread for content that already exists in long-form
- Australian sport/medicine community is small here

### Workshop / conference
- Speaking slots = primary marketing event (2026: Osteopathy Australia conference)
- Conference-only landing page + QR code on last slide

## 12. What's off-limits (compliance & ethical)

- **No clinical advice to non-clinicians.** Public-facing content educates clinicians. We don't tell parents how to assess their kid's concussion.
- **No claims we can't substantiate.** No "proven to reduce malpractice risk", "cures persistent post-concussion syndrome", etc.
- **No misuse of AHPRA/Osteopathy Australia branding.** Endorsement language must follow OA's guidelines.
- **No fake scarcity/urgency.** Real workshop capacity (12) is fine. "Only 3 spots left!" when there are 11 is not.
- **No fake testimonials, fake credentials, fake numbers.** Real clinician brand depends on this — once breached, never recovered.
- **GST + tax invoice compliance** — every sale post-1 May 2026 carries GST. See `lib/tax-invoice.ts`.

## Appendix: Quick reference for content agents

When generating content, every output must:

1. **Be readable by an AHPRA-registered clinician** without dumbing down clinical concepts
2. **Include at least one specific, concrete element** — a number, a named protocol, a clinical scenario, a regulatory reference
3. **Avoid the anti-examples list** in §9
4. **Pass the "would Zac say this?" test** — if it sounds like a marketing agency wrote it, rewrite
5. **End with a clear next step** — implicit (problem named, reader knows what to do) or explicit (link, CTA)

For AI-SEO blog content specifically, also:

6. **Open with a definition block** for the primary query ("What is SCAT6?" → 2-sentence direct answer)
7. **Include a structured comparison or step-by-step block** mid-article
8. **End with an FAQ block** (4–6 questions, schema-ready)
9. **Cite at least 3 sources** with links — peak bodies (AIS, SMA, ACSEP, APA, RACGP, AHPRA), guidelines (ANZ Concussion Guidelines), or peer-reviewed papers
10. **Include author attribution** — Zac Lewis, Osteopath, founder of Concussion Education Australia
