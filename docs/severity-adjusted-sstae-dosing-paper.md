# Severity-adjusted dosing of sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise for concussion

**Scoping document + literature review — working draft**
Concussion Education Australia · drafted 2026-07-06 · lead: Zac Lewis (Osteopath)

> Status: concept + lit review for internal review. Every dose parameter cited to
> primary evidence; guesses are flagged as clinician-judgement, not evidence.
> Thin/uncertain citations are marked ⚠ and must be verified against the primary
> before submission.

---

## 1. The question

Sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise (SSTAE) is first-line care for concussion,
and it is individualised — but **only by intensity**. The Buffalo Concussion
Treadmill Test (BCTT) measures the heart-rate threshold (HRt) at which symptoms
exacerbate, and the patient trains at 80–90% of it. Yet the **starting dose that
sits on top of that band — session duration, weekly frequency, and re-test
cadence — is fixed** (~20 min, most days, weekly re-test) and does **not** vary
with how severe the patient's exercise intolerance is.

A patient who provokes symptoms in the first minute of the BCTT (a near-resting
HRt) receives the same 20 min/most-days prescription as one who tolerates 15
minutes. Clinically this feels wrong; the evidence is **silent** on whether it
is. No published rule titrates the *starting dose* to baseline intolerance
severity.

**Primary aim.** In a prospective multi-site cohort of concussion patients
managed with BCTT-guided SSTAE, describe how exercise tolerance, flare rate,
adherence and HRt recovery-trajectory differ across baseline-severity strata, and
derive a candidate **severity-adjusted starting-dose rule** for external
validation.

## 2. Literature review

### 2.1 SSTAE is established, first-line, and evidence-based

Prescribed early sub-symptom aerobic exercise reduces the incidence of persisting
symptoms and speeds recovery, and is endorsed by the 6th International Consensus
(Amsterdam 2022/23; Patricios et al., *BJSM* 2023). The dose comes from the
Buffalo RCTs, not the consensus (which gives no numbers): exercise at **80% of
the HR at symptom exacerbation** on the BCTT, **~20 min/day**, with a
within-session stop at a **≥2-point** symptom rise on 0–10 (Leddy et al., *JAMA
Pediatrics* 2019; replicated Leddy et al., *Lancet Child & Adolescent Health*
2021). The reviews give the **80–90%** band and progress the dose **by heart rate
(+5–10 bpm/day if tolerated), not by lengthening sessions** (Haider, Leddy et
al., *Sports Health* 2021).

### 2.2 The individualisation is intensity-only — the gap

Two findings define the gap:

1. **The only individualisation is the % -of-HRt band.** Duration is fixed from
   day one; there is no published schedule that titrates starting duration,
   frequency, or re-test interval to severity (Leddy 2019; Haider/Leddy 2021).
   For patients too symptomatic to complete a BCTT, the fallback is a *fixed*
   age-predicted program (≈55%→70% APMHR), keyed to sex and days-since-injury —
   not to measured severity (Popoli, Leddy et al., *CJSM* 2023).

2. **Severity markers exist but are used only prognostically.** An absolute
   **HRt < 135 bpm**, and a **ΔHR (threshold − resting) ≤ 50 bpm** (73%
   sensitive / 78% specific), predict prolonged (>30-day) recovery (Haider et
   al., *Front Neurol* 2019). These identify the harder-to-treat patient but have
   **never been translated into a dose adjustment**.

So the field has a validated way to *flag* severity and a validated way to *set
intensity*, but no bridge between them for the rest of the dose. That bridge is
the contribution.

### 2.3 Adherence is the hidden variable — and it is worse than it looks

Home-exercise-program non-adherence is high: **up to ~65%** are non- or
partially-adherent (Bassett, *NZ J Physiotherapy* 2003). *(⚠ The round "~50%"
figure has no clean primary source — hedge it; cite Bassett as the anchor. Cite
Jack et al., *Manual Therapy* 2010, and Sluijs et al., *Phys Ther* 1993, for
determinants of non-adherence, NOT for a rate.)*

Concussion-specific, the self-report-vs-measured gap is the story:
- **89% adherence by self-report** — but explicitly unobserved (Leddy 2019).
- **61% adherence when objectively HR-verified** at home (Polar OH1), and
  **adherent adolescents recovered faster** (Chizuk et al., *MSSE* 2022).

Adherence is both a confounder and an outcome, and only *measured* adherence is
trustworthy — which is exactly what a wearable-gated platform captures.

### 2.4 The monitoring gap — paper diaries do not work

The canonical evidence that self-report between visits cannot be trusted: with a
hidden photosensor, paper-diary **reported compliance was 90% but actual
compliance 11%**; on 32% of days the diary was never opened yet 92% compliance
was reported (Stone et al., *BMJ* 2002). Wearable/HR verification is the fix and
is only just entering concussion practice (Chizuk 2022).

### 2.5 Digital delivery uses an *estimated*, not *measured*, dose — the wedge

Published app/telehealth concussion aerobic programs prescribe from an
**estimated** target, not a measured BCTT threshold:
- **CARE** — 60% ± 5% of age-predicted max HR (220 − age) (Hutchison et al.,
  *JMIR Formative Res* 2023). *(⚠ confirm 60 vs 55% in the PDF; the method —
  age-predicted — is unambiguous.)*
- **MSTEP** — a fixed HR target (start 10 min @ HR 120 → 60 min @ HR 140), then
  symptom-titrated (Chrisman et al., *Front Pediatr* 2021).
- Estimated-dose RCT paradigm: progressive % of age-predicted max HR (*PLOS One*
  2022, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0276336).

**No published digital tool prescribes from a measured BCTT threshold.** *(⚠ no
PhysiApp/Physitrack-in-concussion aerobic study found.)* That absence is the
platform's genuine wedge and the reason its dataset is novel: it is the first at
scale to pair a *measured* HRt with *verified* session dose.

### 2.6 Precedent: adjacent fields DO titrate the starting dose to severity

Titrating starting dose to baseline severity is standard elsewhere — two models:

- **Test-anchored (% of measured capacity).** Cardiac rehab prescribes intensity
  off a pre-training symptom-limited exercise test, anchored to the individual's
  ventilatory thresholds, with risk-stratified starting conservatism (Mezzani et
  al., EACPR/AACVPR/CACR joint statement, *J Cardiopulm Rehabil Prev* 2012; ⚠
  verify exact intensity tables). Pulmonary rehab sets intensity from baseline
  capacity (>~60% peak work rate) (Spruit et al., ATS/ERS statement, *AJRCCM*
  2013).
- **Tolerance/symptom-titrated within an envelope.** Exercise oncology uses
  start-low/progress-by-tolerance (Campbell et al., *MSSE* 2019). ME/CFS and PEM
  guidance rejects fixed graded increments in favour of a flexible energy
  envelope (NICE NG206, 2021) — a cautionary precedent for *how* to titrate a
  severe subgroup.

Concussion SSTAE is the outlier that does neither for the non-intensity dose.

### 2.7 Synthesis

SSTAE works; the dose is fixed except for intensity; severity is measurable and
prognostic but unused for dosing; adherence is decisive and only trustworthy when
measured; existing digital tools dose from estimates; and adjacent fields already
titrate the starting dose to severity. A platform that measures HRt and verifies
session dose can, for the first time, test whether severity-adjusted dosing
improves tolerance, adherence and recovery in concussion.

## 3. Aims & hypotheses

- **A1 (descriptive).** Characterise tolerance (completed session minutes without
  flare), flare rate, HR-verified adherence, and HRt-trajectory slope across
  baseline-severity strata (threshold stage; HRt <135 vs ≥135; ΔHR ≤50 vs >50).
- **A2 (derivation).** Derive a candidate severity-adjusted starting-dose rule
  (session duration, frequency, re-test cadence) that maximises tolerated dose
  without increasing flare/relapse.
- **A3 (prognostic).** Model time-to-recovery from baseline severity + early
  verified adherence; report per TRIPOD if a prediction model is derived.
- **H1.** Higher baseline severity (early provocation / low HRt / low ΔHR)
  predicts lower tolerated starting dose and higher flare rate at the standard
  fixed dose — i.e. one-size dosing systematically over-doses the severe stratum.

## 4. Methods (sketch)

- **Design.** Prospective, multi-site **observational cohort** embedded in
  routine care across contributing clinics — a **practice-based research
  network** (PBRN) model; precedent: Athletic Training PBRN (*J Athl Train* 2012)
  and a regional-Australia physiotherapy PBRN (PMC10223842). Reported per
  **STROBE** (von Elm et al., *Lancet* 2007).
- **Population.** Concussion/mTBI patients undergoing BCTT-guided SSTAE via the
  platform under clinician oversight.
- **Predictors (baseline severity).** Threshold stage (minute of provocation),
  measured HRt, ΔHR (needs resting HR captured), resting symptom burden.
- **Platform-captured data (the advantage).** Measured HRt; per-session
  HR-verified intensity, duration and symptom deltas; flare events; serial HRt
  trajectories; objectively-measured adherence — none of it self-reported.
- **Outcomes.** Time to symptom resolution / return-to-activity; flare and
  relapse rate; HR-verified adherence; HRt-trajectory slope.
- **Analysis / power.** Stratified description; candidate dosing-rule derivation;
  prognostic modelling sized by **events-per-variable ≥10** (Peduzzi et al., *J
  Clin Epidemiol* 1996) and, preferably, **Riley et al. minimum-sample-size**
  criteria (*Stat Med* 2019; `pmsampsize`). Internal validation
  (bootstrap/optimism); external validation deferred to a second cohort.
- **Safety rail already in product.** Two consecutive flares → rest day + ceiling
  reduction + clinician check-in (implemented 2026-07-06); this is a
  clinician-judgement rail for the same evidence gap the paper addresses, and its
  triggering data is itself an analysable outcome.

## 5. The "founding clinics" contribution & authorship model

Offering clinics a role in the paper is a strong incentive — but it must be done
correctly, because **ICMJE does not permit authorship for supplying data alone.**

- **ICMJE (all four criteria required):** (1) substantial contribution to
  conception/design *or* acquisition/analysis/interpretation; (2) drafting or
  critical revision for intellectual content; (3) final approval; (4)
  accountability. ICMJE states plainly that *"collection of data … alone, does
  not constitute authorship."* A clinic that only submits data is
  **acknowledged**, not an author.
- **The correct mechanism — collaborative group authorship.** Publish "…on
  behalf of the **CEA SSTAE Study Group**." A small core (who meet all four
  criteria) are the accountable named authors; contributing clinicians are listed
  as **named collaborators**, which are **PubMed-indexed and citable** on their
  record — real academic credit, correctly attributed.
- **A tiered, honest offer to founding clinics:**
  - **Data contributor** → named collaborator (Study Group), acknowledged.
  - **Author** → additionally contributes protocol/design input *or* data
    interpretation, **plus** critical revision, final approval, and
    accountability. Offer a defined path (review the protocol, interpret their
    site's data, revise the manuscript) so willing clinicians can genuinely
    qualify — not a purchased byline.
- **Governance (Australia), non-negotiable before any data pools:**
  - **HREC review** under the NHMRC *National Statement* (2023 version operative
    until the 2025 National Statement commences **23 June 2026**); single-review /
    National Mutual Acceptance for multi-site.
  - **Consent, or a waiver** for routine/retrospective clinical data (National
    Statement §2.3.10–2.3.11 criteria: low risk, impracticable to obtain consent,
    no likely objection, privacy protected).
  - **Privacy Act 1988 / APPs** — health data is sensitive information (APP 3, 6,
    11); secondary research use without consent runs through the NHMRC **s95A**
    guidelines; state health-records law may also apply.
  - **De-identification** to OAIC/NHMRC standard before pooling; define
    custodianship, storage location, and access in the protocol.

## 6. Target venue & fit

Consistent with the publications strategy (real outputs, quality over quantity):
a methods/feasibility or cohort paper fits **JMIR / JMIR Formative Research**
(digital-health, where CARE and Rhea published) or **Sports Health / Frontiers in
Neurology** (the Buffalo group's venues). A protocol paper (STROBE/registered)
could precede the cohort to stake the question.
