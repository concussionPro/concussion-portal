> **What this is.** A described, standardised *workflow* — not a trial protocol seeking ethics approval, and not an efficacy claim — for delivering sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise (SSTAE) under clinician supervision using a wearable-delivered, live-monitored digital tool. ## Abstract

**Background.** Early sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise (SSTAE) is an effective, low-risk treatment for sport-related concussion, recommended by the 6th International Consensus Statement (Amsterdam 2022/2023) and supported by two independent randomised controlled trials in adolescents (Leddy et al., *JAMA Pediatr.* 2019; Leddy et al., *Lancet Child Adolesc Health* 2021). Despite this, the treatment is difficult to deliver faithfully, because it depends on an individualised heart-rate ceiling that must be respected on every unsupervised between-visit session, and the structured data needed to progress safely (heart rates achieved, minutes, symptom deltas, next-day flares, serial HRt) is routinely lost outside a research setting.

**Objective.** To specify a standardised, clinician-supervised digital workflow that closes this delivery gap — defining the roles, decision rules, data captured, safety logic, and stopping criteria for faithful between-visit delivery of an established prescription — and to state honestly what the workflow does *not* specify where the evidence is silent.

**Methods.** We operationalise the published SSTAE prescription and the Amsterdam 2023 consensus into a repeatable five-stage sequence, anchoring every stage and every prescription parameter to its primary published source. Prescription parameters (intensity, duration, frequency, within-session stop, re-test cadence, and the prognostic flag) are cited exactly and are not adjusted, ramped, or interpolated beyond what the sources state. The workflow is contrasted with the consumer *pacing* paradigm to make its opposite therapeutic intent explicit.

**Results.** The workflow comprises five stages — (1) baseline and safety screen; (2) graded threshold test on the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT) protocol to a *measured* HRt; (3) individualised band prescription (train at 80–90% of HRt, ~20 minutes, daily/most days, with a within-session stop if symptoms rise ≥2 points on a 0–10 scale); (4) live-monitored between-visit rehabilitation with a within-session stop rule, a structured session log, real-time return of data to the prescriber, and a clinician-reviewable advance/hold/regress/refer/retest decision; and (5) graded return in which serial HRt is the objective recovery curve and a re-test that no longer provokes symptoms surfaces as a clearance-review signal, fed into the consensus return-to-sport/return-to-learn strategy. Three properties distinguish the framework from existing digital delivery: a *measured* (BCTT-derived, not age-predicted-max) HRt anchor; *verification-gated* progression (only live-verified wearable sessions advance the band, which is capped at the measured threshold); and a *fail-closed, no-fabricated-signal* rule.

**Conclusions.** Digital delivery of heart-rate-targeted concussion exercise is not new; the contribution here is **standardisation** — a defined, repeatable, safety-anchored workflow that makes an established prescription deliverable and measurable between visits. The framework makes no effectiveness claim; whether digital delivery improves outcomes over standard prescription is an empirical question deferred to a future out-of-scope study. Its secondary purpose is to generate the structured, de-identified routine-care dataset that such a study would require.

**Trial registration:** Not applicable. **IRRID:** Not applicable.

## 1. Background and rationale

The management of concussion has shifted from rest-until-symptom-free to early, individualised active rehabilitation (Leddy JJ, Haider MN, Ellis M, Willer BS. *Exercise is Medicine for Concussion.* Curr Sports Med Rep. 2018;17(8):262–270). The mechanism is a provocable autonomic/cerebrovascular phenotype — *exercise intolerance* — quantifiable as a heart-rate threshold on a standardised graded test (Leddy JJ, Willer B. *Curr Sports Med Rep.* 2013;12(6):370–376) and treatable by aerobic exercise dosed just below that threshold. The consensus position is relative rest for 24–48 hours, then early prescribed sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise within roughly 2–10 days, with a tolerated *transient* symptom worsening of up to 2 points on a 0–10 scale that settles within about an hour (Patricios JS, Schneider KJ, Dvorak J, et al. *Br J Sports Med.* 2023;57(11):695–711).

The unsolved problem is *delivery fidelity*. The trials worked because participants adhered to an individualised heart-rate ceiling on home sessions under oversight. In routine care the ceiling is invisible to the patient, the home sessions are unobserved, and the structured data needed to progress safely — heart rates achieved, minutes completed, symptom deltas, next-day flares, and serial HRt — is lost. A standardised digital workflow that makes the band *live*, sources heart rate from any wearable, *never fabricates* it, and *reports back* to the prescriber is the instrument to close that gap. This paper specifies that workflow. It is a delivery framework, not an efficacy study.

## 1a. Prior art and the nature of the contribution

This is a *standardisation* paper, and that framing is deliberate, because digital delivery of heart-rate-targeted concussion exercise already exists. We do not claim a first tool:

- Concussion rehabilitation apps already deliver HR-targeted aerobic prescription using **age-predicted maximal HR**: the *Rhea Health* app (a University of Toronto spin-out, founder Michael Hutchison — *not* Buffalo-affiliated) guides users to a **fixed ~60% ± 5% of 220 − age with no graded test**, individualises only through in-app same/better/worse symptom feedback, and reports no dedicated clinician dashboard (Chizuk et al., *JMIR Form Res* 2025;e67275). A separate RCT has delivered early aerobic exercise using a **progressive percentage of age-predicted-max HR** (*PLOS One*, 2022).
- Between-visit mHealth symptom monitoring with structured return of data to clinicians is published (remote patient monitoring of concussed adolescents, 2024).
- The pivotal Buffalo RCTs delivered HRt-based home exercise with **wearable HR monitoring** and serial BCTT re-testing (*JAMA Pediatr.* 2019; *Lancet Child Adolesc Health* 2021) — but these are research methods, not commercial apps.
- A **commercial** concussion exercise-prescription module is in market and reaching Australia (CCMI + Wibbi, Dec 2025), embedding concussion protocols in a home-exercise-program platform with outcome tracking; its BCTT-derived HRt is measured once on paper in clinic, not delivered digitally, and its tracking is of symptom/outcome scores rather than a serial measured-HRt curve.

Against that, the safest and most honest contribution is **not first-ness but standardisation**: defining a single, repeatable, safety-anchored sequence, tied to three specific properties the prior art does not standardise around — a **BCTT-derived (measured) HRt** anchor rather than an age-predicted-max formula; **verification-gated progression** (only live-verified wearable sessions advance the band, which is capped at the measured threshold); and a **fail-closed, no-fabricated-signal** rule (heart rate is shown only when actually measured, else the workflow pauses). The narrow novelty statement carried for the wider programme — *to our knowledge, no commercially available concussion product delivers a measured (BCTT-derived, not age-predicted-max) heart-rate threshold plus live verification-gated heart-rate-zone training plus a serial measured-HRt clinician trajectory in one tool* — is expressed here as a **workflow standard**, which is the lowest-risk form of the claim.

## 2. Design principles

1. **Clinician-owned, not consumer self-managed — a clinician-directed assistant, not a medical device.** A clinician runs/oversees the threshold test, *interprets* it, sets the heart-rate band, owns the prescription, approves each progression, and makes the clearance and return-to-activity decision. The tool only executes, paces, monitors, and records — it **informs; it never decides.** This division positions the workflow as a **clinician-directed rehab-assistance and monitoring/data tool rather than a diagnostic or treatment medical device**, intended to sit within the clinical-decision-support carve-out (FDA 21st Century Cures Act §3060; TGA's CDS exclusion). Each downstream design choice — the clinician setting the band (Stage 3), the never-fabricate-heart-rate rule (principle 3), the symptom-stop and red-flag halt-and-refer paths (principle 5), and progression *proposed for clinician review* rather than applied autonomously (Stage 4) — deliberately supports the non-device argument. It extends the clinician's reach between visits; it does not replace clinical judgement or enable self-diagnosis.
2. **Heart-rate-anchored and individualised on a *measured* threshold.** Every dose is derived from the patient's own HRt from a graded test, not an age-predicted formula, where a graded test is available. (A standardised programme exists for when graded testing is not available — Leddy JJ, et al. *Clin J Sport Med.* 2023 — and can seed the band conservatively.) The percentage-of-HRt band is the *only* individualisation the evidence supports (see §7).
3. **Real signal or no signal — and only verified signal advances the dose.** Heart rate is shown only when actually measured (a real BLE packet, or a confidence-gated camera-PPG estimate used as a *resting spot-check only*); the workflow pauses on "no reading" rather than acting on a guess. Progression is stricter still: only a live-verified wearable session may advance the band, which is capped at the measured HRt. Manual or unverified sessions still count for safety and regression but never raise the ceiling (see Stage 4).
4. **Hardware-agnostic across platforms.** Any wearable broadcasting the standard Bluetooth Heart Rate profile drives the identical workflow — read via Web Bluetooth on Android/desktop and native CoreBluetooth/Android-BLE in the iOS/Android apps. Camera PPG is a resting spot-check only (motion artefact makes it unreliable during exercise), and manual entry is always available (including for Apple Watch and Fitbit, which do not broadcast standard BLE heart rate).
5. **Safety logic travels with the patient.** The within-session symptom-stop rule and the red-flag halt-and-refer path are encoded in the tool, not left to recalled verbal instruction.
6. **Reduce, don't rest.** After a symptom flare, the response is to *continue* daily sub-symptom exercise at *reduced* intensity, not to prescribe a rest day; the only prohibition is sustained supra-threshold exercise (see §5, §7).
7. **Active rehabilitation, not pacing.** The band is a training target to be progressively raised, not an exertion alarm (see §6).

## 3. The five-stage workflow

### Stage 1 — Baseline and safety screen

- **Symptom profile.** The patient selects the post-concussion symptoms they actually experience from the standard 22-item SCAT6/PCSS inventory, so subsequent prompts ask only about relevant symptoms.
- **Readiness / red-flag gate.** Before any exertion, the patient (with clinician oversight) is screened against the SCAT6 emergency red-flag list (severe or worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, focal weakness or numbness, slurred speech, increasing confusion or drowsiness, loss of consciousness). Any positive blocks the test and routes to urgent medical review.
- **Resting symptom score.** A 0–10 baseline is captured immediately pre-test; it is the reference for the provocation criterion.
- **Heart-rate source pairing.** The patient pairs a Bluetooth wearable, enables camera-PPG (resting spot-check only), or the clinician selects manual entry.

*Role:* clinician confirms appropriateness and supervises; patient executes. *Stopping criterion:* any red flag → exit to medical review.

### Stage 2 — Graded threshold test to a measured HRt

A standardised graded ramp modelled on the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), built on the cardiac Balke protocol, increases workload each minute (Leddy JJ, Willer B. *Curr Sports Med Rep.* 2013;12(6):370–376). The published BCTT procedure is:

- **Start.** Treadmill at a fixed walking speed set by height — **3.2 mph** for patients ≤5′10″ and **3.6 mph** for patients >5′10″ — at **0° incline for the first minute**.
- **Ramp.** Increase incline by **1 degree per minute** (not per cent) up to a maximum of **15°**; once 15° is reached, hold the incline and increase **speed by 0.4 mph per minute**.
- **Monitoring.** Record heart rate, rate of perceived exertion (Borg **6–20**), and a **0–10** symptom/VAS score **every minute**.
- **HRt definition.** HRt is the heart rate at the first stage at which the symptom score rises **≥3 points above the resting baseline** — the validated diagnostic provocation criterion. *(Note the deliberate distinction from the ≥2-point within-session TRAINING stop of Stage 3/4 — the two thresholds are different and must not be conflated.)*
- **Terminations.** The test terminates on any of: a **≥3-point VAS rise** (symptom-limited → HRt obtained); **voluntary exhaustion (RPE >17)** without symptom provocation; reaching **≥90% of age-predicted maximum heart rate (220 − age)**; rapid clinical deterioration; or patient request.
- **Cool-down.** 2.0 mph, 0° incline, 2 minutes.

**Three terminations, three meanings:**
- *Symptom-limited* → HRt obtained; interpretation **physiologic** (exercise intolerance present → proceed to prescription).
- *Voluntary exhaustion without provocation* (RPE >17, no ≥3-point rise) → interpretation **no-intolerance**: symptoms unlikely to be exercise-driven; redirect the workup (cervicogenic / vestibular / mood). On a *re-test*, this is the recovery/clearance signal.
- *Red flag* → halt and refer; no HRt is produced from an unsafe stop.

*Role:* clinician interprets the termination. *Output:* HRt (bpm) + interpretation, synced to the clinic record as the first point on the recovery curve.

**Prognostic flag (recorded, not a dose input).** At presentation, an absolute **HRt <135 bpm** together with a blunted heart-rate response (**ΔHR — threshold minus resting — ≤50 bpm**) predicts prolonged (>30-day) recovery, with roughly **73% sensitivity and 78% specificity** (Haider MN, Leddy JJ, et al. *Front Neurol.* 2019;10:395). This is surfaced to the clinician as a prognostic signal only; it does **not** alter the prescribed dose.

### Stage 3 — Individualised band prescription

The concussion prescription, taken directly from the cited literature:

- **Intensity.** Train at **80–90% of HRt**. The **80% floor** was the intensity used in the pivotal RCT (Leddy JJ, Haider MN, Ellis MJ, et al. *JAMA Pediatr.* 2019;173(4):319–325); the **80–90% band** is the operational range in the practice literature (Leddy JJ, Haider MN, Hinds AL, Willer B. *Clin J Sport Med.* 2021;31(2):e89–e94; Haider MN, Leddy JJ, Willer B, et al. *Sports Health* 2021, PMC8167349). The **upper bound is an explicit do-not-exceed ceiling**; the lower bound guarantees a therapeutic stimulus.
- **Duration.** **~20 minutes per day, fixed from day one** — the session length is *not* ramped up over the programme. (This is a common point of error: progression is by heart rate, not by lengthening the session — see Stage 4.)
- **Frequency.** **Daily** in the RCTs; **5–7 days/week** ("most days") clinically. There are **no scheduled rest days**.
- **Within-session stop rule (encoded for Stage 4).** Stop the session if symptoms rise **≥2 points above the pre-session score** on the 0–10 scale (Leddy JJ, et al. *JAMA Pediatr.* 2019; consensus tolerance band, Patricios et al. 2023). This ≥2-point *training* threshold is deliberately lower than, and distinct from, the ≥3-point *diagnostic* threshold of Stage 2.
- Conservative presets exist for moderate/severe TBI and other neuro conditions (wider safety margin, lower/narrower band, shorter sessions); the *safety logic is identical*, only the dose defaults change, and these presets are *not* validated by the concussion RCTs.

*Output:* a plain-language prescription the patient can follow and a structured band (low/high bpm) the tool enforces live.

### Stage 4 — Live-monitored between-visit rehabilitation

This is the stage no paper prescription can deliver.

- **Live band feedback.** During each home session the measured heart rate is shown against the band in real time as *under / in-zone / over*, so the patient self-doses to an otherwise invisible ceiling.
- **Within-session stop rule.** If symptoms rise ≥2 points above pre-session, the tool prompts the patient to stop — encoding the consensus tolerance band rather than relying on recall.
- **Structured session log.** Each session records average and peak heart rate, completed minutes, pre- and peak-symptom scores, and a next-day flare flag.
- **Return to the prescriber.** When the patient entered a clinic code, every threshold test and training session syncs to the clinician dashboard (HRt trajectory, band, minutes, symptom deltas, flares). While a session is running, a short-TTL live tick lets the clinician watch in real time and drops the patient off the live view automatically when they stop. Self-guided patients (no code) transmit nothing.
- **Progression — by heart rate, not by duration, and clinician-reviewable.** In the RCTs, progression is by **heart rate**: the target ceiling is raised by roughly **+5–10 bpm** when the patient tolerates the current dose without provocation, while the session stays at ~20 minutes (Leddy JJ, et al. *JAMA Pediatr.* 2019; *Lancet Child Adolesc Health* 2021). The workflow operationalises this as a clinician-reviewable proposal of *advance / hold / regress / refer / retest* from the recent session history:
  - **Advance** the ceiling (+5–10 bpm) after a run of clean **live-verified** sessions (no within-session provocation, no next-day flare). Only sessions whose heart rate was live-verified against a broadcasting wearable count toward the clean run; manual or camera-PPG sessions never advance the band. *(The requirement for a "run" of verified clean sessions and a minimum-minutes completion gate are an added digital conservatism, not a parameter specified by the RCTs — see §7.)*
  - **Regress — reduce, don't rest.** On recent repeated provocation, reduce the ceiling and continue daily sub-symptom exercise at the *reduced* intensity — the response to a flare is a lower dose, not a rest day; the only prohibition is sustained supra-threshold exercise. Regression is *never* gated: flare/safety evidence counts from every session, verified or not.
  - **Cap at the measured threshold.** An advance never exceeds the measured HRt; once the band reaches it the tool proposes a **re-test** rather than ratcheting higher — a fresh graded test is the only safe way to raise the band.
  - **Hold** otherwise.
  A recency-windowed regression prevents an old, resolved flare from ratcheting the ceiling down indefinitely once the patient is running clean.
- **Re-test cadence.** Re-test the HRt on a graded test **weekly** in the RCTs; **1–2 weekly** clinically.

*Role:* patient executes; clinician monitors and approves progression. *Stopping criteria:* red flag at any point; repeated provocation → regress/refer.

### Stage 5 — Graded return

- **Serial HRt is the objective recovery curve.** Re-testing at intervals tracks the threshold rising toward (or reaching) no-provocation; the BCTT is reliable as a repeated measure (Leddy JJ, Baker JG, Kozlowski K, Bisson L, Willer B. *Clin J Sport Med.* 2011;21(2):89–94), which is what licenses serial testing as a recovery trajectory.
- **Clearance signal.** A re-test that returns *no-intolerance* (voluntary exhaustion, RPE >17, without provoking symptoms) surfaces automatically as a clinician clearance-review flag.
- **Integration with the consensus return strategy.** Clearance to progress aerobic load is then fed into the consensus graded **Return-to-Sport** and **Return-to-Learn** strategy (Patricios et al., 2023) — symptom-limited stepwise progression through activity stages with the same ≤2-point tolerance rule — which remains a clinician-led decision. The digital workflow supplies the objective aerobic-tolerance evidence; it does not, by itself, clear an athlete for contact.

## 4. Roles, data, and governance

- **Prescriber (clinician).** Confirms suitability, oversees the threshold test, owns the prescription, reviews progression, makes the clearance and return-to-sport decisions.
- **Patient.** Executes home sessions inside the band, logs symptoms, reports flares.
- **Data captured.** HRt and serial re-tests; the prognostic HRt/ΔHR flag; prescribed band; per-session average/peak HR, minutes, pre/peak symptom scores, next-day flare; interpretation labels; timestamps.
- **Data flow.** Patient → clinic (only with a clinic code), best-effort and non-blocking; live ticks are ephemeral (short TTL), session history is durable. (Patient consent and clinic data-contribution terms are specified in the companion data/consent document; that companion is kept to *clinical-care* purpose to avoid tripping a prospective-research classification — see §7.)

## 5. Decision rules at a glance

| Situation | Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Red flag at screen or mid-test | SCAT6 emergency list positive | Halt, no HRt, urgent medical review |
| Symptom rise ≥3 pts above rest during graded test | Diagnostic provocation criterion | Record HRt at that stage; prescribe band |
| RPE >17 / ≥90% age-predicted max, no ≥3-pt rise (initial) | No exercise-intolerance phenotype | Redirect workup (cervical/vestibular/mood) |
| RPE >17, no ≥3-pt rise (re-test) | Recovered tolerance | Flag clearance review; graded return |
| HRt <135 bpm AND ΔHR ≤50 bpm | Prognostic flag (recorded, not a dose input) | Surface likely-prolonged-recovery signal to clinician |
| Symptom rise ≥2 pts during a home session | Within-session TRAINING stop rule | Stop session; log flare |
| Repeated recent provocation | Reduce, don't rest | Regress ceiling, continue daily at reduced intensity; consider re-test/referral |
| Run of clean **verified** sessions | Tolerance improving (verified sessions only) | Advance ceiling +5–10 bpm, capped at measured HRt |
| Band reaches measured HRt | Ceiling cap | Prompt re-test; never advance past the measurement |

## 6. Positioning: active rehabilitation vs consumer pacing

A growing class of consumer apps uses wearable or camera-PPG heart-rate/HRV data to help people with ME/CFS and long-COVID **pace** — that is, stay *under* an energy/heart-rate ceiling to avoid post-exertional malaise (the most prominent being Visible, which likewise estimates heart-rate/HRV from a phone camera and pairs an arm-worn monitor). In those conditions, *avoiding* exertion is the therapeutic goal, and the ceiling is an alarm.

This framework is the deliberate inverse. For the concussion exercise-intolerance phenotype the evidence supports *training* — a prescribed aerobic stimulus delivered *within* a band whose upper edge sits just below the symptom-provocation threshold, with the explicit intent to **progressively raise that band as tolerance recovers** (Leddy et al., 2019; 2021). The heart-rate band is therefore a **therapeutic target**, not an exertion ceiling; "in-zone" is the goal, "under" is under-dosing, and "over" is the only state shared with pacing. Same wearable substrate, opposite therapeutic intent. The distinction is clinically load-bearing: applying a pacing/avoidance mindset to a patient whose recovery depends on a graded aerobic stimulus would withhold the treatment. The workflow is built so the framing — train within and raise the band, under clinician progression — is explicit at every step.

## 7. Scope, limitations, evidence gaps, and the evidence pathway

This is a *delivery framework*, not an efficacy study. It standardises faithful delivery of an established prescription; it does not itself establish a new clinical outcome. The strongest underlying evidence is in adolescents with early sport-related concussion; adults and persistent-symptom populations are supported but less strongly.

**Explicit evidence gaps — what this framework does NOT specify.** Where the literature is silent, the framework does not invent a rule. Four gaps are named honestly rather than filled:

1. **There is no validated severity-adjusted *starting* dose.** The percentage-of-HRt band is the *only* individualisation the evidence supports. A more cautious starting point for a highly symptomatic patient (e.g. a shorter first session, or starting at the low end of the band) is *clinician judgement*, not an app default, and the tool must not present it as evidence-derived. Deriving a validated severity-adjusted starting dose is a distinct future research question, out of scope here.
2. **The RCTs specify no next-day symptom-contingent rule.** The framework records a next-day flare flag and lets the clinician act on it, but the trials did not prescribe a specific "if next-day symptoms, then X" dose adjustment; the reduce-don't-rest response is drawn from the general management principle, not from a trial-specified next-day algorithm.
3. **There is no published protocol for a large or delayed flare.** Management of a substantial or late-onset symptom spike (as distinct from the transient ≤2-point within-session worsening the consensus tolerates) is not specified in the literature; the workflow routes such events to clinician review rather than encoding an unvalidated automated response.
4. **There is no specified washout between the diagnostic BCTT and program start.** The evidence does not define an interval between the threshold test and the first training session; the framework leaves this to clinician timing.

**Digital-delivery conservatisms are design choices, not evidence.** The requirement for a *run* of clean *live-verified* sessions before an advance, the minimum-minutes completion gate, the specific bpm increment chosen within the +5–10 bpm range, and the verification-gating of progression are added conservatisms of this workflow, not parameters the RCTs specify. They are stated as design decisions, not as findings.

**Secondary purpose — the future evidence pathway.** The framework's secondary purpose is to generate a structured, de-identified routine-care dataset (serial HRt, adherence, time-to-clearance, flares) that could power a **future retrospective observational study** — the appropriate, low-burden vehicle for a real-world effectiveness signal, requiring only de-identified-data ethics review rather than a prospective interventional trial. **One critical caveat on that pathway:** the low-burden de-identified-data review applies **only if the data arose from routine clinical care and is analysed retrospectively.** If the tool's data capture is instead designed *for the purpose of research* — collected prospectively with research consent baked into the clinic/in-app terms — a reviewer or HREC may treat it as **prospective research requiring full HREC review and prospective consent**, forfeiting the low-burden pathway. This classification question must be resolved with a research-ethics advisor **before** any research-data capture is built (see `04-venues.md` and `06-data-consent-copy.md`). That study is explicitly **out of scope** for this framework paper, which collects no patient data and makes no effectiveness claim.

**Regulatory scope.** This framework describes a **clinician-directed rehab-assistance and monitoring/data tool, not a diagnostic or treatment medical device.** The clinician makes every clinical decision (interpretation, band, progression, clearance) and the software only executes, paces, monitors, and records — the basis for positioning it within the clinical-decision-support carve-out (FDA 21st Century Cures Act §3060; TGA's CDS exclusion). This positioning *reduces but does not guarantee* non-device status — claims and intended use drive classification — so a SaMD scoping opinion should be obtained before launch.
