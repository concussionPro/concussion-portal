# A standardised clinician-supervised digital workflow for sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise after concussion

**Author:** Zac Lewis, Registered Osteopath (AHPRA-registered), B.Clin.Sci, M.Ost.Med
**Affiliation:** Concussion Education Australia Pty Ltd, 2 Wordsworth St, Byron Bay NSW 2481, Australia
**ORCID:** 0009-0002-4267-0451

## Abstract

The 2022 Amsterdam international consensus made sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise (SSTAE), prescribed from an individually measured heart-rate threshold (HRt) derived by a graded exercise test, a first-line treatment for concussion. Translating that prescription into practice requires a graded test, an individualised heart-rate band, monitored between-visit sessions, and progression governed by heart rate rather than session duration. This protocol specifies a standardised five-stage clinician-supervised workflow for delivering SSTAE with digital monitoring. The clinician interprets the test, sets the band, approves each progression, and makes all clearance decisions; the tool executes, paces, monitors and records. Two distinct symptom thresholds are used and must not be conflated: a rise of 3 points or more defines the diagnostic threshold during graded testing, and a rise of 2 points or more is the within-session training stop rule.

## Guidelines

**Intended users.** Clinicians supervising concussion rehabilitation, and the patients they prescribe for. This workflow is clinician-directed throughout: it informs, it never decides.

**Two symptom thresholds - do not conflate.**

- **3 points or more** above resting baseline = the **diagnostic** provocation criterion during the graded test (Stage 2).
- **2 points or more** above pre-session score = the **within-session training stop rule** during home sessions (Stages 3-4).

**Reduce, don't rest.** After a symptom flare the response is to continue daily sub-symptom exercise at *reduced* intensity, not to prescribe a rest day. The only prohibition is sustained supra-threshold exercise.

**Progression is by heart rate, not duration.** Sessions remain ~20 minutes throughout. The ceiling rises; the session length does not.

**Real signal or no signal.** Heart rate is displayed only when actually measured. The workflow pauses on "no reading" rather than acting on an estimate. Only live-verified wearable sessions may advance the band.

## Safety warnings

**SCAT6 emergency red flags - halt and refer immediately at any stage.** Severe or worsening headache; repeated vomiting; seizure; focal weakness or numbness; slurred speech; increasing confusion or drowsiness; loss of consciousness. Any positive finding blocks testing and routes to urgent medical review. No HRt is produced from an unsafe stop.

**Do-not-exceed ceiling.** The upper bound of the prescribed band is an explicit do-not-exceed ceiling. An advance never exceeds the measured HRt.

**Scope limit.** This workflow supplies objective aerobic-tolerance evidence. It does not, by itself, clear an athlete for contact. Clearance and return-to-sport remain clinician-led decisions under the consensus graded strategy.

## Materials

- Treadmill (or cycle ergometer where a standardised alternative protocol is used)
- Heart-rate monitor broadcasting the standard Bluetooth Heart Rate profile
- Borg rate of perceived exertion scale (6-20)
- 0-10 symptom/visual analogue scale
- SCAT6/PCSS 22-item symptom inventory
- SCAT6 emergency red-flag list

# Procedure

**Stage 1: Baseline and safety screen**


### Step 1. Record the symptom profile (Stage 1)

The patient selects the post-concussion symptoms they actually experience from the standard 22-item SCAT6/PCSS inventory, so that subsequent prompts ask only about relevant symptoms.

### Step 2. Screen against the red-flag gate (Stage 1)

**SAFETY - perform before any exertion.** Screen the patient, with clinician oversight, against the SCAT6 emergency red-flag list: severe or worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, focal weakness or numbness, slurred speech, increasing confusion or drowsiness, loss of consciousness.

**Stopping criterion:** any positive finding blocks the test and routes to urgent medical review.

### Step 3. Capture the resting symptom score (Stage 1)

Record a 0-10 baseline immediately pre-test. This is the reference value for the provocation criterion in Stage 2.

### Step 4. Pair the heart-rate source (Stage 1)

The patient pairs a Bluetooth wearable, enables camera photoplethysmography (resting spot-check only - motion artefact makes it unreliable during exercise), or the clinician selects manual entry.

**Note.** Apple Watch and Fitbit do not broadcast standard BLE heart rate; use manual entry for these devices.

**Role:** clinician confirms appropriateness and supervises; patient executes.

---

**Stage 2: Graded threshold test to a measured HRt**


A standardised graded ramp modelled on the Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), built on the cardiac Balke protocol.

### Step 5. Set the starting workload (Stage 2)

Treadmill at a fixed walking speed set by height: **3.2 mph** for patients 5 ft 10 in or under, **3.6 mph** for patients over 5 ft 10 in. Hold **0 degrees incline for the first minute**.

### Step 6. Ramp the workload each minute (Stage 2)

Increase incline by **1 degree per minute** - degrees, not per cent - to a maximum of **15 degrees**. Once 15 degrees is reached, hold the incline and increase **speed by 0.4 mph per minute**.

### Step 7. Monitor every minute (Stage 2)

Record heart rate, rate of perceived exertion (Borg 6-20), and a 0-10 symptom/VAS score **every minute**.

### Step 8. Identify the heart-rate threshold (HRt) (Stage 2)

HRt is the heart rate at the first stage at which the symptom score rises **3 points or more above the resting baseline** - the validated diagnostic provocation criterion.

**Do not conflate** the 3-point diagnostic threshold with the 2-point within-session training stop rule used in Stages 3-4.

### Step 9. Terminate the test on any termination criterion (Stage 2)

Terminate on any of:

- a **VAS rise of 3 points or more** (symptom-limited, so HRt is obtained)
- **voluntary exhaustion (RPE above 17)** without symptom provocation
- reaching **90% or more of age-predicted maximum heart rate** (220 minus age)
- rapid clinical deterioration
- patient request

### Step 10. Interpret the termination (Stage 2)

Three terminations carry three distinct meanings:

- **Symptom-limited**: HRt obtained; interpretation **physiologic** (exercise intolerance present). Proceed to prescription.
- **Voluntary exhaustion without provocation** (RPE above 17, no rise of 3 points or more): interpretation **no-intolerance** — symptoms unlikely to be exercise-driven. Redirect the workup (cervicogenic / vestibular / mood). On a *re-test*, this is the recovery/clearance signal.
- **Red flag**: halt and refer. No HRt is produced from an unsafe stop.

**Role:** clinician interprets the termination.

### Step 11. Cool down (Stage 2)

2.0 mph, 0 degrees incline, 2 minutes.

### Step 12. Record the prognostic flag (Stage 2)

At presentation, an absolute **HRt below 135 bpm** together with a blunted heart-rate response (**delta-HR (threshold minus resting) of 50 bpm or less**) predicts prolonged (over 30-day) recovery, with approximately **73% sensitivity and 78% specificity**.

**This is recorded and surfaced to the clinician as a prognostic signal only. It does not alter the prescribed dose.**

**Output:** HRt (bpm) plus interpretation, recorded as the first point on the recovery curve.

---

**Stage 3: Individualised band prescription**


### Step 13. Set the intensity band (Stage 3)

Train at **80-90% of HRt**. The 80% floor was the intensity used in the pivotal randomised controlled trial; the 80-90% band is the operational range in the practice literature.

**SAFETY:** the upper bound is an explicit do-not-exceed ceiling. The lower bound guarantees a therapeutic stimulus.

### Step 14. Set the duration (Stage 3)

**Approximately 20 minutes per day, fixed from day one.** Session length is **not** ramped up over the programme.

**Common point of error:** progression is by heart rate, not by lengthening the session. See Stage 4.

### Step 15. Set the frequency (Stage 3)

**Daily** in the randomised controlled trials; **5-7 days per week** ("most days") clinically. There are **no scheduled rest days**.

### Step 16. Encode the within-session stop rule (Stage 3)

Stop the session if symptoms rise **2 points or more above the pre-session score** on the 0-10 scale. This 2-point *training* threshold is deliberately lower than, and distinct from, the 3-point *diagnostic* threshold of Stage 2.

**Note on other populations.** Conservative presets exist for moderate/severe traumatic brain injury and other neurological conditions (wider safety margin, lower and narrower band, shorter sessions). The safety logic is identical; only the dose defaults change. These presets are **not** validated by the concussion randomised controlled trials.

**Output:** a plain-language prescription the patient can follow, plus a structured band (low/high bpm) enforced live during sessions.

---

**Stage 4: Live-monitored between-visit rehabilitation**


### Step 17. Deliver live band feedback during each home session (Stage 4)

Display the measured heart rate against the prescribed band in real time as *under / in-zone / over*, so the patient can self-dose to an otherwise invisible ceiling.

### Step 18. Apply the within-session stop rule (Stage 4)

If symptoms rise 2 points or more above the pre-session score, prompt the patient to stop. This encodes the consensus tolerance band rather than relying on recalled verbal instruction.

### Step 19. Log the session (Stage 4)

Record average and peak heart rate, completed minutes, pre-session and peak symptom scores, and a next-day flare flag.

### Step 20. Return data to the prescriber (Stage 4)

Where the patient has entered a clinic code, every threshold test and training session syncs to the clinician record: HRt trajectory, band, minutes, symptom deltas, flares. Self-guided patients without a clinic code transmit nothing.

### Step 21. Progress by heart rate - clinician-reviewable (Stage 4)

Progression in the randomised controlled trials is by **heart rate**: raise the target ceiling by approximately **+5-10 bpm** when the patient tolerates the current dose without provocation, while the session remains at ~20 minutes. Propose *advance / hold / regress / refer / retest* from recent session history for clinician review:

- **Advance** the ceiling (+5-10 bpm) after a run of clean **live-verified** sessions - no within-session provocation, no next-day flare. Only sessions whose heart rate was live-verified against a broadcasting wearable count toward the clean run. Manual or camera-PPG sessions never advance the band.
- **Regress - reduce, don't rest.** On recent repeated provocation, reduce the ceiling and continue daily sub-symptom exercise at the reduced intensity. The response to a flare is a lower dose, not a rest day. Regression is **never** gated: flare and safety evidence counts from every session, verified or not.
- **Cap at the measured threshold.** An advance never exceeds the measured HRt. Once the band reaches it, prompt a **re-test** rather than ratcheting higher - a fresh graded test is the only safe way to raise the band.
- **Hold** otherwise.

Apply a recency window to regression so that an old, resolved flare does not ratchet the ceiling down indefinitely once the patient is running clean.

**Note.** The requirement for a *run* of verified clean sessions, and a minimum-minutes completion gate, are an added digital conservatism - not parameters specified by the randomised controlled trials.

### Step 22. Re-test on cadence (Stage 4)

Re-test the HRt on a graded test **weekly** in the randomised controlled trials; **1-2 weekly** clinically.

**Role:** patient executes; clinician monitors and approves progression.
**Stopping criteria:** red flag at any point; repeated provocation, regress or refer.

---

**Stage 5: Graded return**


### Step 23. Track the objective recovery curve (Stage 5)

Serial re-testing tracks the threshold rising toward, or reaching, no-provocation. The BCTT is reliable as a repeated measure, which is what licenses serial testing as a recovery trajectory.

### Step 24. Surface the clearance signal (Stage 5)

A re-test returning **no-intolerance** - voluntary exhaustion, RPE above 17, without provoking symptoms - surfaces automatically as a clinician clearance-review flag.

### Step 25. Integrate with the consensus return strategy (Stage 5)

Feed clearance to progress aerobic load into the consensus graded Return-to-Sport and Return-to-Learn strategy: symptom-limited stepwise progression through activity stages with the same 2-point tolerance rule.

**SAFETY - scope limit.** This remains a clinician-led decision. The digital workflow supplies the objective aerobic-tolerance evidence; it does not, by itself, clear an athlete for contact.

# Expected results and troubleshooting

## Decision rules at a glance

**Red flag at screen or mid-test**
: *SCAT6 emergency list positive* - Halt, no HRt, urgent medical review

**Symptom rise of 3 pts or more above rest during graded test**
: *Diagnostic provocation criterion* - Record HRt at that stage; prescribe band

**RPE above 17 / 90%+ age-predicted max, no rise of 3 pts or more (initial)**
: *No exercise-intolerance phenotype* - Redirect workup (cervical/vestibular/mood)

**RPE above 17, no rise of 3 pts or more (re-test)**
: *Recovered tolerance* - Flag clearance review; graded return

**HRt below 135 bpm AND delta-HR 50 bpm or less**
: *Prognostic flag (recorded, not a dose input)* - Surface likely-prolonged-recovery signal

**Symptom rise of 2 pts or more during a home session**
: *Within-session TRAINING stop rule* - Stop session; log flare

**Repeated recent provocation**
: *Reduce, don't rest* - Regress ceiling, continue daily at reduced intensity; consider re-test or referral

**Run of clean verified sessions**
: *Tolerance improving (verified sessions only)* - Advance ceiling +5-10 bpm, capped at measured HRt

**Band reaches measured HRt**
: *Ceiling cap* - Prompt re-test; never advance past the measurement


## Known limitations and evidence gaps

1. **There is no validated severity-adjusted starting dose.** The percentage-of-HRt band is the only individualisation the evidence supports. A more cautious starting point for a highly symptomatic patient is clinician judgement, not an evidence-derived default.
2. **The trials specify no next-day symptom-contingent rule.** This workflow records a next-day flare flag and lets the clinician act on it; the reduce-don't-rest response derives from the general management principle, not a trial-specified next-day algorithm.
3. **There is no published protocol for a large or delayed flare.** Management of a substantial or late-onset symptom spike - as distinct from the transient within-session worsening of 2 points or less the consensus tolerates - is not specified in the literature. Route such events to clinician review rather than encoding an unvalidated automated response.
4. **There is no specified washout between the diagnostic graded test and programme start.** The evidence does not define an interval; this is left to clinician timing.
