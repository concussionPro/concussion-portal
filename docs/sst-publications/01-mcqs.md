# Multiple-choice questions

Sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise for concussion: from the Buffalo test to digital, clinician-supervised delivery — a Narrative Review. Zac Lewis.

**1. During a Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (BCTT), the heart-rate threshold (HRt) is defined as the heart rate at the point where:**

a) The patient reaches 85% of age-predicted maximal heart rate
b) Symptoms rise by 3 or more points above the pre-test rating
c) The rating of perceived exertion exceeds 17
d) The treadmill reaches its maximum incline

**Answer: b.** The BCTT is symptom-limited: the validated provocation criterion is a symptom rise of ≥3 points from rest, and the heart rate at that minute is the HRt (Section 2.1). Voluntary exhaustion at high RPE without symptom provocation yields no HRt — it indicates the absence of exercise intolerance.

**2. Under the 6th International Consensus Statement (Amsterdam), during sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise a symptom increase is acceptable when it is:**

a) Absent — any symptom rise should stop the session
b) Up to 2 points on a 0–10 scale, settling within about an hour
c) Up to 5 points, regardless of duration
d) Any magnitude, provided heart rate stays within the prescribed band

**Answer: b.** The consensus specifies a mild, transient worsening of up to 2 points on a 0–10 scale as acceptable provided it settles within about an hour; the within-session stop rule is therefore a rise of more than 2 points (Sections 1.3 and 2.5).

**3. In the 2021 multicentre randomised controlled trial in adolescents (Lancet Child & Adolescent Health), early targeted-heart-rate aerobic exercise compared with placebo-like stretching:**

a) Made no difference to outcomes
b) Roughly halved the risk of persistent post-concussive symptoms
c) Increased the rate of adverse events
d) Was effective only when started more than 30 days after injury

**Answer: b.** The trial reported a 48% reduced risk of persistent post-concussive symptoms (hazard ratio for stretching versus aerobic exercise 0.52, 95% CI 0.28–0.97), with good adherence and no adverse events (Section 2.3, Table 1).

**4. Which finding on initial graded exercise testing predicted delayed (>30-day) recovery in adolescents managed without aerobic exercise (Haider et al., 2019)?**

a) A heart-rate threshold above 160 bpm
b) A change in heart rate of ≤50 bpm across the test
c) A resting heart rate below 60 bpm
d) A symptom rise of exactly 2 points during the test

**Answer: b.** A blunted heart-rate response — ΔHR ≤50 bpm across the test — predicted delayed recovery with roughly 73% sensitivity and 78% specificity in the rest and placebo-stretching arms (Section 2.2).

**5. In the one completed randomised trial of sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise in adults with persisting post-concussive symptoms (ACTBI, 2025), the exercise programme:**

a) Significantly reduced symptom burden compared with stretching
b) Was poorly tolerated by exercise-intolerant participants
c) Produced no significant between-group difference in symptom burden, with quality of life improving in some analyses
d) Halved time to recovery, replicating the adolescent trials

**Answer: c.** The adult randomised evidence is thinner and more equivocal: symptom burden did not differ significantly between groups; the programme was tolerated, and quality of life improved in some analyses (Sections 2.3 and 5, Table 1).
