# The design principle

Every module delivers **real, complete, usable value** — this is genuine CPD, not a teaser, because the endorsement bodies (CIMSPA, REPS, SESNZ) need it to be real and because a bait-and-switch would fail an AHPRA-minded audience.

But the value it delivers is **recognition and safe referral — not management.** The course makes a clinician genuinely competent to *recognise concussion and act safely*, and equally makes it **obvious they are not yet competent to manage it.** That gap is stated plainly, not implied, because it is true: recognition and management are different competencies.

The learner finishes able to say: *"I can spot it, I know rest is out and exercise is in, and I understand what modern management involves — but I cannot yet measure a threshold, prescribe the dose, monitor recovery, or clear a return to play."* That final sentence is the paid course.

**The rule for every module:** give the whole of the recognition/safety competence away. At the exact point where *management* begins — measuring, dosing, progressing, clearing — stop, name what is required, and point to the full course.

# The four modules

## Module 1 — Concussion care has changed
**VALUE (given in full):** The paradigm shift. Until 2022, concussion was a rest condition; the Amsterdam consensus made early sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise a *first-line treatment*. The evidence: Leddy 2019, median recovery 13 vs 17 days (n=103, P=.009). Most clinicians were trained on the rest model — the 1,272-physiotherapist survey found 70% already trained, but current to the old era.
**THE GAP (made obvious):** "Knowing the treatment changed is not the same as being able to deliver it." Sets up the whole course.
**Cannibalize:** CRM Module 1 ("Why Exercise Is Now First-Line").
**This is the hook** — most clinicians do not know the treatment changed, and finding out creates the professional itch the rest of the course scratches.

## Module 2 — Recognise, red flags, and your scope
**VALUE (given in full — this is the real, complete competence):** How to recognise concussion; the emergency red flags and what to do; the boundary of your scope; when to refer. A clinician finishes this module genuinely competent to recognise and refer safely. **Nothing is withheld here** — this is the safe floor every clinician should have, and it is what the endorsement bodies are endorsing.
**THE GAP:** "Recognising it and knowing rest is wrong is where your current training ends. Managing it is a separate skill set." Explicit.
**Cannibalize:** CRM Module 2 + the Schools "Recognise & Remove" plain-language content.

## Module 3 — The modern pathway, and where your competence ends
This module reveals **two distinct specialist competencies** the learner does not have — because concussion care now splits into two jobs done by two professions, and the free course routes each learner to the right paid course.

**VALUE (understanding, not capability):** the shape of the full modern pathway —
- **Diagnosis and clinical management** (the CCM job — physios, OTs, osteopaths, GPs): it is not enough to recognise concussion; you must *diagnose* it, identify the *phenotype* driving it, manage persistent symptoms, coordinate the team, and own the *return-to-play clearance* decision.
- **Exercise delivery** (the CRM job — exercise physiologists): the first-line treatment is a sub-symptom-threshold aerobic prescription dosed off an individually **measured** heart-rate threshold, delivered between visits and progressed by re-testing.

**THE GAP (the conversion point — split by role):**
- To the diagnosing clinician: *"Recognition is not diagnosis. Phenotyping the injury, managing PPCS, and clearing a safe return is clinical management this briefing does not teach."* → **CCM**.
- To the exercise professional: *"Understanding that exercise is first-line is not the same as being able to measure a threshold, prescribe the dose, and progress it safely."* → **CRM**.

**Stop here.** Do not teach diagnostic phenotyping, the BCTT method, the band calculation, or the progression rules. Reveal *what* each competency is; withhold *how*.
**Cannibalize:** CCM diagnosis/phenotype/PPCS modules (the diagnosis reveal) + CRM Modules 3–4 (the exercise reveal) — the *what* and *why* from each, never the *how*.

## Module 4 — Return to work, school and play
**VALUE:** that return is a *graded clinical decision* tied to objective recovery, not a fixed timeline; a plain-language overview of the staged model. Relevant to both markets, and to the funder — return-to-work is exactly what ACC measures.
**THE GAP:** "Reading exercise tolerance to stage the return, and making and documenting the clearance decision defensibly, is clinical management — the diagnosing clinician owns the clearance (CCM); the exercise professional supplies the objective tolerance evidence that informs it (CRM)."
**Cannibalize:** CCM Return-to-Play/Work/School module — the framework, not the decision protocol.

# The closing device — the competence statement

End the course with an explicit, honest competence summary. This is the carrot, and it works *because* it is true:

> **You are now equipped to:** recognise concussion, respond to red flags, refer appropriately, and understand that modern care means active diagnosis and an exercise-based first-line treatment.
>
> **You are not yet equipped to** (and the course routes the learner to the relevant line):
> - *If you diagnose and manage patients* — phenotype the injury, manage persistent symptoms, and make and document a safe return-to-play clearance. **→ Concussion Clinical Mastery (CCM).**
> - *If you deliver exercise rehabilitation* — measure a symptom-limited heart-rate threshold, prescribe and progress a sub-symptom training dose, and monitor recovery between visits. **→ Concussion Rehab Mastery (CRM).**

The end-of-course call-to-action **branches by role**: "I diagnose and manage → CCM" / "I deliver exercise rehab → CRM." Detect or ask the learner's profession at enrolment so the reveal in Module 3 and the CTA speak to the right gap.

A certificate is issued for the free course. It should state exactly what it certifies — *recognition and safe referral* — so it is honest on a CPD record and so the gap to "diagnosis/management" or "exercise delivery" is visible on the document itself.

3. **In-person full course** — hands-on practical + OSCE competency (AU/NZ audiences).

# Honest targeting note

The free course feeds two different paid products for two different buyers — match the channel to the product:

- **Diagnosis/management market → CCM.** Physios, OTs, osteopaths, GPs. Channels: SEPNZ, OTNZ-WNA, the NZ ACC supplier clinicians, the AU grad list.
- **Exercise market → CRM.** Exercise physiologists. Channels: ACSM, CEP-UK, CASES, CSEP, HPCSA/biokineticists.

It converts **clinicians who can act on the gap** in either market. For **PT-level endorsement audiences** (CIMSPA, REPS) the free course delivers reach, a listing and brand credibility, but weak conversion — neither diagnosis nor clinical exercise delivery is in their scope, so neither upgrade applies. Treat those listings as distribution, not a revenue target.

**One product decision for you:** the free course must serve two masters — the endorsement bodies want "recognise-screen-refer safely" (Modules 1–2 deliver exactly that) and the funnel wants the management gap revealed (Modules 3–4). They are compatible in one course. But confirm each endorsing body will accept a course whose back half is "here is what you cannot yet do" — most will, because Modules 1–2 alone satisfy a safety-CPD remit, and 3–4 read as "further learning available." If any body wants a self-contained course with no upsell, give them Modules 1–2 only as the endorsed unit.
