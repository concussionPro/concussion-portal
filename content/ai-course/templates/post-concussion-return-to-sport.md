## How this plan works

Recovery from concussion is best managed through a graduated, symptom-limited progression. Each stage requires at least 24 hours symptom-free at the current activity level before progressing.

If symptoms return at any stage, step back one stage, rest 24 hours, then re-attempt the previous stage.

This plan should be followed in conjunction with a graduated return-to-learn or return-to-work plan if relevant.

---

## Stage 1 — Symptom-limited activity

**What you can do:**
- Daily activities that don't worsen symptoms
- Walking and light activities of daily living
- Reduced screen use as tolerated

**What to avoid:**
- Exercise that elevates heart rate substantially
- Contact sport
- Activities requiring complex decision-making for sport

**Time at this stage:** At least 24-48 hours, until symptoms are stable.

---

## Stage 2 — Light aerobic exercise

**What you can do:**
- Walking, stationary cycling at low intensity (under 70% maximum heart rate)
- 10-15 minutes initially, building as tolerated
- No resistance training yet

**What to avoid:**
- Head impact
- Resistance training
- Sport-specific drills

**Criteria to progress:** Tolerates light aerobic exercise without symptom worsening for at least 24 hours.

---

## Stage 3 — Sport-specific exercise (no head impact)

**What you can do:**
- Running drills relevant to your sport
- Skill drills that don't involve head impact (e.g. passing, kicking, controlled handling)
- Light resistance training

**What to avoid:**
- Head impact
- Body contact
- High-risk manoeuvres (heading, scrums, tackling drills)

**Criteria to progress:** Tolerates sport-specific exercise without symptom worsening for at least 24 hours.

---

## Stage 4 — Non-contact training drills

**What you can do:**
- Full non-contact training with your team
- Progressive resistance training
- More complex training drills, including coordination and cognitive load (passing under pressure, decision-making drills)

**What to avoid:**
- Body contact
- Tackling, heading, scrums

**Criteria to progress:** Tolerates full non-contact training without symptom worsening for at least 24 hours. Medical clearance required before progressing to Stage 5.

---

## Stage 5 — Full-contact practice

**What you can do:**
- Full participation in normal training, including contact
- Following written medical clearance only

**Criteria to progress:** Tolerates full-contact training without symptom worsening for at least 24 hours.

---

## Stage 6 — Return to play

Full return to competitive play, including contact, after written medical clearance and successful completion of Stages 1-5.

---

## Important notes

- **Minimum total return-to-play time after a single concussion is generally 7-14 days for most adults**, but full recovery may take longer for some individuals and is longer for adolescents and children.
- For paediatric and adolescent athletes, current guidance recommends more conservative timelines and full return-to-learn before full return-to-play.
- A second concussion before full recovery can result in serious harm. If symptoms recur, withdraw immediately.
- Sport governing bodies may have their own mandatory protocols (AFL, NRL, Rugby Australia, FA, school sport associations) — these must be followed in addition to this plan.

## Red flags requiring immediate medical review

- Worsening headache that doesn't improve
- Vomiting
- Increasing confusion or drowsiness
- Visual disturbance
- Seizure
- Weakness or numbness in arms or legs
- Any symptom that worries you or your family

For severe symptoms, call 000 or attend the nearest emergency department.
