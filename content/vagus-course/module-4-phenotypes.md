# Module 4 — Phenotypes Worth Knowing

---

Most "vagal" presentations cluster into five distinct phenotypes. Recognising which one you're looking at changes the workup, the prognosis, and the referral pathway.

[KEYPOINT: The clinical question is not "is this vagal?" — it's "which autonomic phenotype is this?" Different phenotypes need different referrals.]

[INFOGRAPHIC: autonomic-phenotype-matrix]

## 1. POTS (Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome)

**Pattern:** HR increase ≥30 bpm within 10 min of standing, sustained ≥10 min, without orthostatic hypotension. Symptoms: lightheadedness, palpitations, fatigue, brain fog.

**Demographics:** Predominantly female (4:1), onset usually 13-50 years. Often post-infectious or post-concussive trigger.

**Workup:** Cardiology referral with autonomic interest. Tilt-table testing or supervised active stand. Rule out hyperthyroidism, anaemia, hypovolaemia.

**In-house intervention while awaiting referral:** Conservative — increase salt + fluids, compression stockings, avoid sudden positional changes. Defer exercise prescription until cardiology has cleared.

[BREAK]

## 2. Inappropriate Sinus Tachycardia (IST)

**Pattern:** Resting HR ≥100 bpm with average 24-hour HR ≥90 bpm, without identifiable cause. Patient often unaware until palpitations are noted incidentally.

**Workup:** Cardiology referral. ECG, Holter, exclude thyroid, anaemia, deconditioning.

**Do not:** Intervene independently. IST can be confused with POTS, hyperthyroidism, anxiety-driven tachycardia. Refer first.

[BREAK]

## 3. Post-Concussion Autonomic Dysfunction

**Pattern:** Following mild TBI, new orthostatic intolerance, exercise intolerance (symptoms reproduced at sub-threshold exertion), HR-variability disturbances on activity.

**Workup:** Concussion-specialist referral if available. Otherwise GP-led with neurology backup. Buffalo Concussion Treadmill Test (Leddy et al.) is the gold-standard exercise tolerance evaluation.

**In-house intervention:** Graded sub-symptom-threshold aerobic exercise (Buffalo protocol) has RCT support — see references. This is one of the few defensible vagal-adjacent interventions.

## 4. Long COVID Autonomic Cluster

**Pattern:** Post-acute COVID, new orthostatic intolerance, fatigue, exercise intolerance, "brain fog." Often meets POTS criteria on tilt-table or active stand.

**Workup:** GP-led with cardiology + long-COVID-clinic referral where available. Rule out other post-acute COVID sequelae (cardiac, pulmonary).

**In-house intervention:** Conservative. Pacing protocols, gradual reconditioning, salt/fluid loading. Avoid intensive exercise prescription until cardiology cleared.

[REDFLAG: New exertional symptoms in a long-COVID patient warrant cardiology workup BEFORE any exercise prescription. Post-viral myocarditis is a real differential.]

[BREAK]

## 5. Anxiety-Driven Autonomic Activation

**Pattern:** Episodic tachycardia, lightheadedness, gastrointestinal symptoms, that correlate with anxiety triggers. Often pre-dates the patient's vagal-framing of the problem.

**Workup:** GP-led mental-health assessment. Standard tools (DASS-21, K10). Exclude organic causes first.

**In-house intervention:** Psychological-therapy referral. Slow-paced breathing as a self-management adjunct has good evidence — Module 5.

[TRYTHIS: When you suspect anxiety-driven autonomic activation, ask: "Does this happen at predictable times, or in response to specific situations?" If yes — anxiety-driven. If no, and symptoms occur on standing, position change, or exertion — physiological.]
