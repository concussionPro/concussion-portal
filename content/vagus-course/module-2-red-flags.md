# Module 2 — Red Flags & Differentials

---

Vagal-framed symptoms have a high false-positive rate. The presenting complaint of *"dizzy, lightheaded, GI upset, feeling off"* maps to vagal-coded language easily — but maps just as easily to cardiac, endocrine, and neurological pathology you cannot afford to miss.

This module is the medicolegal scaffolding for the rest of the course.

[KEYPOINT: A "vagal symptom" diagnosis is acceptable only after you've ruled out the differentials that look identical at the bedside. The wellness ecosystem skips this step. You cannot.]

[INFOGRAPHIC: red-flag-decision-tree]

## The four red-flag categories

### 1. Cardiac

- New-onset palpitations + dizziness in a patient over 50, or with a family history of sudden cardiac death
- Syncope on exertion (not on standing) — exertional syncope is cardiac until proven otherwise
- Chest discomfort accompanying autonomic symptoms
- Bradycardia <40 bpm or tachycardia >150 bpm at rest

**Action:** GP referral for ECG ± Holter monitoring before any vagal-framed intervention.

[BREAK]

### 2. Endocrine

- Heat intolerance + weight loss + tachycardia → thyroid storm differential
- Cold intolerance + bradycardia + fatigue → hypothyroidism
- Episodic flushing + hypertension + headache → phaeochromocytoma (rare but unmissable)
- Diabetes with autonomic neuropathy presents as orthostatic intolerance

**Action:** TFTs, electrolytes, glucose. Refer for endocrinology if pattern fits.

[BREAK]

### 3. Neurological

- New focal neurological signs (numbness, weakness, visual changes) alongside autonomic complaints
- Multi-system autonomic failure pattern (orthostatic + bladder + bowel + sweating) → consider MSA, parkinsonism
- Recent head injury + new autonomic symptoms → post-concussion autonomic dysfunction (Module 4)
- Acute autonomic dysreflexia in spinal cord injury patients

**Action:** Neurological examination, GP/neurology referral. Never default to a vagal-tone intervention if focal neurology is present.

[BREAK]

### 4. Pharmacological

- Beta blockers, SSRIs/SNRIs, antipsychotics, antihistamines, anticholinergics all affect autonomic function
- Recent medication start or dose change is the first thing to ask about
- Recreational substances (THC, MDMA, stimulants) acutely alter autonomic balance

**Action:** Full medication history. Many "autonomic" presentations resolve on med review.

[REDFLAG: Three signs you should not own a vagal-framed presentation in-house: focal neurological signs, exertional syncope, chest pain alongside autonomic symptoms. Refer same-day.]

## The clinical question to lead with

Before any vagal-framed assessment or intervention, the question is: **what would I have to be wrong about for this patient to die or be seriously harmed?**

If the answer involves cardiac, neurological, or endocrine pathology — refer first, intervene second.

[TRYTHIS: Add a single-line prompt to your intake: *"Has anything triggered this recently? Recent medications, infections, injuries, or stressful events?"* The answer reframes most "vagal" presentations within 30 seconds.]
