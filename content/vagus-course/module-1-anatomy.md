# Module 1 — Anatomy & Evidence Essentials

---

> **Important — this module is education, not a clinical decision-support tool.** Vagus-nerve discourse contains an unusually high ratio of marketing to evidence. This course is designed to anchor your clinical reasoning in the literature, not the wellness ecosystem.

The vagus nerve is the tenth cranial nerve and the longest of the autonomic nervous system. It is the dominant parasympathetic output of the body, providing efferent innervation from the medulla to most thoracic and abdominal viscera, and carrying ~80% afferent fibres back to the brainstem from the same territory.

[KEYPOINT: ~80% of vagal fibres are AFFERENT — sending information FROM the periphery TO the brain. The popular "vagus nerve = relaxation output" framing inverts the actual traffic direction.]

## What the literature actually supports

The neuroanatomy is uncontested. Two efferent motor nuclei in the medulla — the dorsal motor nucleus (DMN) and the nucleus ambiguus (NA) — give rise to two distinct fibre populations:

- **Dorsal motor nucleus (DMN) fibres** — unmyelinated, slow-conducting, project to most thoracoabdominal viscera below the diaphragm.
- **Nucleus ambiguus (NA) fibres** — myelinated, fast-conducting, project primarily to cardiac and laryngeal targets above the diaphragm.

[DEFINITION: DMN | Dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus — the older, unmyelinated efferent population in the medulla. Provides parasympathetic output to most viscera below the diaphragm.]

[DEFINITION: NA | Nucleus ambiguus — the myelinated efferent population in the medulla. Provides faster parasympathetic output, dominantly to cardiac targets.]

This dual-output anatomy is the basis of contemporary autonomic physiology and is the part of "polyvagal theory" that genuinely traces back to the literature.

## What the polyvagal-marketing ecosystem claims

The wellness and self-help adaptation of polyvagal theory — sometimes called *"polyvagal-informed practice"* — has propagated claims that go meaningfully beyond what the underlying anatomy supports.

Common claims you will hear from patients (and some practitioners):

- That a "ventral vagal state" is a measurable physiological state
- That specific exercises *"tone the vagus nerve"* in a reliable, reproducible way
- That HRV-based devices can accurately track *"vagal state"* in real time
- That trauma is stored *"in the vagus nerve"* and released through specific manoeuvres

The published evidence for most of these claims is, at best, preliminary. The anatomy is real. The interventions are mostly aspirational.

[REDFLAG: A patient who frames their entire history around "low vagal tone" or "dysregulated vagal state" has been pattern-matched by the wellness ecosystem. Treat the underlying clinical question (POTS? Long COVID? Anxiety? Autonomic dysfunction post-concussion?) — not the vagal-tone framing.]

## What you can defensibly claim

A defensible clinical position on the vagus nerve in 2026:

- Vagal anatomy is real and well-characterised.
- Vagal tone — measured as HRV — has loose correlations with health outcomes, but the causal direction is unclear.
- Some interventions (slow-paced breathing, vagal manoeuvres for tachyarrhythmia) have meaningful evidence in specific contexts.
- Most "vagal-tone training" interventions for general wellness lack RCT support.
- Patients framing chronic illness through a "vagal" lens may have underlying autonomic dysfunction that deserves proper triage.

The rest of this course operates from that position.

[TRYTHIS: Next time a patient mentions vagal tone or polyvagal-state, ask: "What symptoms are you actually experiencing, day to day?" The conversation that follows is the clinical conversation. The vagal framing is downstream.]
